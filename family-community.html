Family & Community

Help families understand, shape, and celebrate the work.

Families are important partners in responsible AI use, student support, transparency, privacy, and community sharing.

What students will do

Learn about learning, then design for a real need.

Students will study how people learn, how AI systems differ from human reasoning, and how AI can be used responsibly. They will identify a specific STEM learning challenge, select an evidence-based strategy, create an AI-supported learning aid, test it, and revise it.

The goal is not to have AI do schoolwork for students. The goal is to design tools that help learners think, practice, explain, reflect, and receive useful feedback.
Family partnership

Ways families can participate

Share learning experiences

Help students identify authentic challenges and describe what kinds of support have or have not been useful.

Review privacy and transparency

Ask what information the tool collects, how AI is used, what limitations exist, and how adults remain involved.

Test and give feedback

Try a student prototype, describe what was clear or confusing, and suggest revisions.

Join a showcase

Attend a school, virtual, or Hunter College sharing event and celebrate student learning and design.

Support responsible use

Discuss when AI helps learning and when it may replace important thinking or practice.

Help improve the program

Provide feedback on communication, accessibility, student support, safety, and the value of the club experience.

Privacy and safety

What families should expect

  • Students should not enter unnecessary personal or sensitive information into AI tools.
  • Student work should not be uploaded or shared without appropriate permission.
  • AI outputs should be reviewed for accuracy, bias, and appropriateness.
  • Students should understand that AI can make mistakes and does not replace teachers or caregivers.
  • School policies, platform requirements, and age restrictions should be reviewed before use.
  • Student tools should be designed with accessibility and respectful feedback in mind.
Community sharing

Possible event formats

Family night

Students demonstrate tools, explain their learning goals, and gather family feedback.

Virtual showcase

Students from different schools share projects through short presentations and guided feedback.

Hunter College event

Students, teachers, families, researchers, and community partners gather for demonstrations, mentoring, and celebration.

Questions families can ask

Conversation starters

  1. What is the learner trying to understand or do?
  2. What specific challenge did the student identify?
  3. What learning strategy is the tool using?
  4. What does the AI do, and what must the learner still do?
  5. How were privacy, safety, and accuracy considered?
  6. What changed after testing?
