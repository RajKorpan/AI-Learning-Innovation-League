Build and Technology Guide

Choose an appropriate platform and build a prototype that is safe, reviewable, and aligned with the learning strategy.

Students do not need advanced coding experience. The first goal is to create enough of the interaction to test whether it supports learning.

Choose a prototype format

Four valid ways to build

No-code AI tool

Use a school-approved platform that allows students to define prompts, roles, instructions, and sharing settings without programming.

Use when: student accounts are permitted and the team needs a working interaction.

General AI assistant

Test a structured prompt in an approved AI assistant. Keep examples non-sensitive and save prompt versions separately.

Use when: the team is testing prompt quality rather than building a separate application.

Storyboard or scripted conversation

Show what the AI and learner would say at each step. Another student or advisor can role-play the AI.

Use when: accounts are unavailable, the design is early, or school policy limits access.

Clickable mockup or coded prototype

Create a simple interface or application when students have the time, support, and technical background.

Use when: the learning interaction is already clear and the added technology serves a testable purpose.

Platform selection

Review the tool before using it

AreaQuestions to answer
AccountsWho creates the account? What age restrictions apply? Is teacher-managed access available?
DataWhat prompts, files, conversations, or identifiers are stored or used?
PrivacyCan the team avoid personal information? Are conversations public, private, or retained?
ControlCan students define instructions, limits, refusals, feedback rules, and interaction steps?
AccessibilityDoes the platform support keyboard use, screen readers, captions, zoom, clear contrast, and alternative input?
CostIs the free version sufficient? Are paid features required for saving, sharing, or advanced controls?
School compatibilityIs the platform approved, blocked, or restricted by local policy?
FallbackWhat paper, storyboard, or advisor-managed alternative will be used if access fails?
Minimum viable prototype

What Version 1 must include

  • Intended learner and learning goal
  • Specific learning challenge
  • Selected learning strategy
  • AI role and interaction sequence
  • Feedback and hint rules
  • What the AI must not do
  • Privacy, accuracy, and accessibility safeguards
  • At least one realistic test case
Build process

Move through versions deliberately

Version 1: Interaction

Check whether the sequence makes sense and whether the learner still has to think.

Version 2: Learning support

Improve strategy alignment, feedback, hint timing, and usability.

Version 3: Safeguards

Address privacy, accuracy, bias, accessibility, and remaining limitations.

Common build problems

How to revise them

ProblemRevision
The AI gives the answer immediatelyRequire an attempt, explanation, or prediction before feedback.
The interaction is too open-endedAdd a clear sequence, one question at a time, and stopping points.
Feedback is genericAsk the AI to identify one strength, one gap, and one targeted next step.
The AI repeats itselfAdd state or progress rules and specify how to respond after repeated errors.
The prompt requests unnecessary informationRemove personal details and use only the learning information required.
The platform is inaccessibleProvide an alternate format or choose a different tool.
