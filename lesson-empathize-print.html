Session 1 · Empathize

Understand the learner

Back to session

Session purpose

Help students begin with a real learner—including themselves—rather than a technology idea. Students learn a small amount about human learning and generative AI, then listen for what is actually difficult before proposing solutions.

Student stage: Empathize

Materials

  • student devices or printed interview worksheet
  • paper or sticky notes
  • index cards for quick build
  • timer
  • approved AI tool only for optional practice

Before the session

Try the activity yourself, read the short explanation, and decide what adaptations your students may need.

Facilitator preparation

Try the student activity

  • Try two short decisions your students will face: separating learning from task completion and asking neutral questions before jumping to a solution.
  • Choose one, then check what you notice.
  • Think of something you learned recently. Name one piece of evidence that you could use the knowledge or skill again—not merely that you finished the task.
  • Choose one, then check what you notice.

Why this matters

  • Where did you want to jump to a solution?
  • What information did you realize you were missing?
  • What might a student need modeled rather than merely explained?
  • Learning is a change in knowledge, skill, strategy, or understanding that can be used again. Generative AI can suggest hypotheses or possibilities, but it cannot tell you what a particular learner needs without evidence from the learner and task.

Plan your support

  • Choose one broad learning complaint you can use as an example. Decide how you will model a neutral follow-up question and how students can participate without disclosing sensitive information.

Student meeting: 1 × 45-minute meeting

Quick build + two essentials 0–7 minutes

What to do

  1. Open Student Step 2 and run the two-minute build. Keep it intentionally rough.
  2. Ask one question: “What did your quick build make the learner do?”
  3. Give the one-minute learning/AI explanation below. Do not add a separate mini-lecture.

Student website step: Student Step 2 · Make something fast

What to explain

Learning evidence + AI limits

1-minute explainer: Finishing one task is not the same as learning. AI can suggest examples and possibilities, but it cannot tell you what a real learner needs without evidence from that learner.

Try saying: “We are going to use AI as a helper, but the real person is our evidence. First we listen.”

Example: A learner may copy a worked answer correctly but still be unable to explain or use the idea on a new problem.

Watch for: Do not let students turn the quick build into their project idea.

Students produce: one rough build and one idea about what real learning evidence would look like

Question Detective 7–12 minutes

What to do

  1. Open Student Step 3. Let students choose an answer before you explain anything.
  2. Run the two short clues. After each choice, ask: “What did this question assume?”
  3. Point students to the small question bank and move on. They do not need to master interviewing first.

Student website step: Student Step 3 · Question Detective

Students produce: a small bank of neutral starter questions

Plan the interview 12–20 minutes

What to do

  1. Open Student Step 4 and have students choose who they will interview.
  2. Students name the STEM topic/task and choose 3–5 starter questions from the bank.
  3. Have them add one question of their own only if they have a genuine question they want answered.
  4. If a student is nervous, open the optional 3-minute AI rehearsal. Otherwise skip it.

Student website step: Student Step 4 · Plan + conduct the interview

Support/challenge options:
Need more support? Rehearse one minute

Use this when: a student freezes, writes leading questions, or is anxious about the interview

  1. Use only the three question stems shown on the student page.
  2. Role-play one question and one follow-up with the facilitator.
  3. If needed, use the optional structured AI rehearsal for no more than about three minutes.

Materials: student page; optional approved AI tool

Ready for more? Add one comparison question

Use this when: the student is already comfortable interviewing

  1. Add “When is this easier?”
  2. Ask what is different in that easier situation.
  3. Do not infer the reason yet.

Materials: interview plan

Students produce: a short interview plan ready to use

Conduct the real interview 20–35 minutes

What to do

  1. Students conduct the real interview with their teacher, another learner, or structured self-observation.
  2. Remind them to ask for a specific example when they hear an important claim.
  3. Keep notes short: what the person said, what they did, and what they already tried.
  4. If the interview cannot happen during the meeting, students schedule/complete it before moving fully into Define.

Student website step: Student Step 4 · Conduct the real interview

Students produce: brief notes from a real interview or structured self-observation

Build the Empathy Map 35–42 minutes

What to do

  1. Open Student Step 5 immediately after the interview.
  2. Students use short phrases to fill the six Empathy Map boxes: trying to do, what they do now, where it seems hard, what they tried, direct clues, and open questions.
  3. If they are unsure where it gets hard, they should write that uncertainty rather than invent an explanation.
  4. Generate the map and read it aloud once.

Student website step: Student Step 5 · Build your Empathy Map

Support/challenge options:
Need more support? Use exact words first

Use this when: students start interpreting before recording evidence

  1. Copy one short phrase from the interview into “What did you hear or see?”
  2. Fill “Trying to do” from the interview topic.
  3. Leave “Where it seems hard” as “I’m not sure yet” if needed.

Materials: interview notes + Empathy Map

Ready for more? Add another perspective

Use this when: students have a clear first map and time remains

  1. Ask one second person or examine one work sample.
  2. Mark one clue that agrees and one that differs.
  3. Carry the difference into Define as an open question.

Materials: second perspective or work sample

Students produce: a saved Empathy Map that can carry into Define

Facilitator check 42–45 minutes

What to do

  1. Ask students to show the Empathy Map, not explain a proposed solution.
  2. Ask which parts came directly from the interview and which parts are still a best guess.
  3. Confirm that they have at least one open question to investigate in Define.

Student website step: Student Step 6 · Check together

Students produce: an Empathy Map grounded in real clues and ready for Define

Student meeting: 2 × 45-minute meetings

Session 1 — 45 minutes

Quick build + two essentials 0–8 minutes

What to do

  1. Run the two-minute quick build.
  2. Ask what the quick build made the learner do.
  3. Give the one-minute learning/AI explanation; keep it tied to the build.

Student website step: Student Step 2 · Make something fast

What to explain

Learning evidence + AI limits

1-minute explainer: A finished task is not automatically evidence of learning, and AI does not know a particular learner without evidence from that learner.

Try saying: “Use AI for possibilities; use the learner for evidence.”

Example: A copied solution may be correct even when the learner cannot explain the idea.

Watch for: Keep this to about one minute.

Students produce: one rough build + one statement about learning evidence

Question Detective 8–15 minutes

What to do

  1. Run the two Question Detective clues.
  2. Let students change their selection after feedback.
  3. Have them choose 3–5 starter questions from the question bank.

Student website step: Student Step 3 · Question Detective

Students produce: 3–5 neutral starter questions

Plan the interview 15–25 minutes

What to do

  1. Choose who to interview and name the real STEM task.
  2. Select starter questions and add one student-generated question if useful.
  3. Check that none of the questions names a cause or solution.

Student website step: Student Step 4 · Plan + conduct the interview

Students produce: a short interview plan

Optional rehearsal or live model 25–30 minutes

What to do

  1. Use this only for students who need it.
  2. Either role-play one minute with the facilitator or use the structured 3-minute AI practice.
  3. Ask the student to name one question they will keep and one they would change.

Student website step: Student Step 4 · Optional practice

Students produce: confidence to begin the real interview

Begin the real interview 30–45 minutes

What to do

  1. Students begin the real interview.
  2. Ask for examples and note what the person says/does.
  3. Stop before interpreting causes.

Student website step: Student Step 4 · Conduct the real interview

Students produce: first-round interview notes

Session 2 — 45 minutes

Finish the interview or observe one attempt 0–15 minutes

What to do

  1. Finish the interview if needed, or watch one real attempt at the learning task.
  2. Ask one follow-up about what happened right before the difficult moment.
  3. Record exact words/actions before interpreting them.

Student website step: Student Step 4 · Conduct the real interview

Students produce: complete interview/observation notes

Build the Empathy Map 15–30 minutes

What to do

  1. Fill the six map areas with short phrases from the interview.
  2. Mark uncertainty honestly.
  3. Generate the visual Empathy Map.

Student website step: Student Step 5 · Build your Empathy Map

Support/challenge options:
Need more support? Sort evidence before interpreting

Use this when: students mix what they saw with what they think caused it

  1. Underline direct quotes/actions.
  2. Circle interpretations separately.
  3. Put only direct clues in the evidence box.

Materials: notes + two colors

Ready for more? Compare two perspectives

Use this when: a team has a strong first map

  1. Add a second learner/teacher/work sample.
  2. Mark one agreement and one difference.
  3. Carry the difference into Define as a question.

Materials: second perspective

Students produce: a complete Empathy Map

Turn uncertainty into questions 30–40 minutes

What to do

  1. Read the map aloud.
  2. Ask: “What do we know directly?” and “What are we still guessing?”
  3. Write 1–3 questions that Define should investigate.

Student website step: Student Step 5 · Build your Empathy Map

Students produce: open questions for Define

Facilitator check 40–45 minutes

What to do

  1. Check that the map is grounded in real interview/observation clues.
  2. Check that the team has not chosen a final solution yet.
  3. Confirm one question they will investigate next.

Student website step: Student Step 6 · Check together

Students produce: Empathy Map approved as a starting point for Define

What students should have at the end

  • An interview plan with open, neutral starter questions.
  • Notes from the real interview or structured self-observation, with observations separated from interpretations.
  • Empathy clues: repeated patterns, something observed directly, one assumption that changed, and open questions to carry into Define.
  • No final solution selected yet.

Keep the project moving without adding another full session

  • Complete any learner or teacher interview that did not fit in the meeting.
  • Have students save exact observations separately from interpretations.
  • Ask students to bring one sample task, attempt, or example into Define when appropriate.
