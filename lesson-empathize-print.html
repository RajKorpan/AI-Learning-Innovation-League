Session 1 · Empathize

Understand the learner

Back to guide

Mission

Help students listen to a real learner or teacher, capture useful clues, and resist jumping to a solution too soon.

Key question: What is the learner trying to do, and what seems to make it hard?

Before the session

  • Know who students will interview and make sure participation is appropriate.
  • Review Student Steps 2–5 so you know what students will see.
  • Have paper or sticky notes, a timer, and a device or printed interview sheet ready.
  • Plan a non-sensitive alternative for students who should not discuss a personal learning experience.

Watch for

  • Leading questions that suggest the answer or cause.
  • Students jumping from one comment directly to a product idea.
  • Treating an AI-generated guess as evidence about the learner.
  • Recording identifying or sensitive information that is not needed.

If students get stuck

  • “Ask what happened, not what you think caused it.”
  • “What did you actually hear or see?”
  • “What is one thing you are still unsure about?”

Activities · 1 × 45-minute meeting

Make something fast0–5

Let students complete the rough two-minute build. Keep it playful and unfinished; its job is to get them making immediately.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#quick-build

Students produce: a rough warm-up build

Question Detective5–10

Let students choose first. After feedback, ask what the stronger question avoids assuming. Then move on; they do not need to master interviewing.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#neutral-question-game

Students produce: a small bank of neutral starter questions

Plan the interview10–17

Students choose who they will interview, name the STEM task, and select a few starter questions. Use the optional short practice only when a student needs it.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-coach

Students produce: a short interview plan

Conduct the real interview17–32

Students ask the questions and follow up on what they hear. Encourage note-taking in short phrases instead of trying to interpret everything during the conversation.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-coach

Students produce: interview notes based on what was actually said or observed

Build the Empathy Map32–40

Students sort their notes into what the learner is trying to do, what they do now, where it seems hard, what has been tried, interview clues, and open questions.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-clues

Students produce: a completed Empathy Map

Check together40–45

Ask students to separate what they know from what they are only guessing. Do not approve a diagnosis; approve a useful question to investigate next.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#advisor-check

Students produce: one clear best guess plus an open question

Activities · 2 × 45-minute meetings

Quick build + Question DetectiveSession 1 · 0–8

Run the rough build and two-question challenge. Keep both fast.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#quick-build

Students produce: a rough build and neutral question starters

Plan the interviewSession 1 · 8–20

Students select starter questions and rehearse only if they need support.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-coach

Students produce: an interview plan

Begin the real interviewSession 1 · 20–40

Students conduct the real interview or observation. Let the conversation breathe rather than rushing to complete every question.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-coach

Students produce: first interview notes

Capture loose endsSession 1 · 40–45

Ask students to write one useful clue and one question they still have.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-clues

Students produce: one clue and one open question

Finish interviewing or observingSession 2 · 0–15

Finish the real interview, or use the time to clarify one important point with the teacher/learner.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-coach

Students produce: complete interview notes

Build the Empathy MapSession 2 · 15–32

Students organize the interview evidence into the visual map.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-clues

Students produce: a completed Empathy Map

Compare evidence and guessesSession 2 · 32–40

Ask which parts come directly from the interview and which are still hypotheses.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#interview-clues

Students produce: a clearly labeled best guess to investigate

Check togetherSession 2 · 40–45

Confirm students are ready to move into Define without prematurely selecting a solution.

Student activity: student-empathize.html#advisor-check

Students produce: facilitator-approved Empathy Map

What students should produce

  • Interview plan
  • Interview notes
  • Empathy Map
  • One best guess to investigate
  • One open question to carry into Define

Quick check

  • Can students say what the learner is trying to do?
  • Can they point to something they actually heard or observed?
  • Can they distinguish evidence from a guess?
  • Are they still investigating the problem rather than defending a solution?
