What students will do

Students work as a team to understand a real learning challenge and create one learning tool for other learners. The final tool may use AI, may be created with help from AI, or may not use AI at all.

  1. Learn: Study how people learn, how AI works and sometimes fails, and how to use technology responsibly.
  2. Listen: Interview a teacher-client and learners about something that is difficult to teach or learn.
  3. Define and research: Narrow the challenge to one specific component and investigate learning-science evidence.
  4. Design and build: Compare several ideas and create a simple prototype, such as cards, a game, a guide, a prompt, a Gem, a chatbot, or an application.
  5. Test and improve: Observe people using the tool, identify what works or causes confusion, and revise it.
  6. Present: Share the project and demonstrate the tool at a presentation event.
One shared project: Students generally create one team product. The goal is not for every student to build an individual AI application.

How families can help

Before the project

  • Read the program information and ask questions about participation.
  • Share any accessibility, language, technology, schedule, or support needs with the advisor.
  • Help the student understand expectations for interviews, teamwork, responsible technology use, testing, and presentation.

During the project

  • Ask the student to explain the learning problem and what the team is discovering.
  • Encourage the student to listen, verify information, share responsibilities, and revise after feedback.
  • Test a prototype or give feedback when invited and appropriate.
  • Do not select or build the solution for the team.

Before the presentation event

  • Help the student practice explaining the project in clear language.
  • Ask what the tool helps the learner do, what evidence supports it, and what limitations remain.
  • Review confirmed event details and request any needed accommodations.

At and after the event

  • Attend when possible and ask students about what they learned.
  • Provide feedback on clarity, usefulness, accessibility, safety, and student support.
  • Celebrate thoughtful research, teamwork, testing, and revision—not only technical complexity.
