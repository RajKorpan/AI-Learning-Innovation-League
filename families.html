Families and community

Family and Community Guide

Families are partners in building the League. They provide feedback on transparency, privacy, safety, accessibility, value, student support, and events—not only attend at the end.

Project purpose

Students investigate authentic learning challenges, study how people learn, design evidence-informed learning tools, test them with users, and improve them through feedback. The final tool may or may not use AI.

What students will experience

  • Learn about human learning, AI, and common misconceptions.
  • Interview a teacher-client and peer learners.
  • Analyze a specific learning challenge.
  • Research credible learning-science evidence.
  • Create one team learning tool.
  • Test, revise, reflect, and participate in a showcase.
  • Possibly be invited to provide feedback about the program; details will be explained separately.

How AI will and will not be used

AI mayAI will not be treated as
Support research, brainstorming, examples, practice, feedback, simulation, or reflection.An automatic authority on learning, teaching, disability, placement, or ability.
Help build a Gem, chatbot, or application when approved.A requirement for every final product.
Be studied for errors, bias, privacy, and limitations.A replacement for teachers, families, or learner thinking.

Privacy

  • Students should not enter names, contact information, grades, schedules, medical information, passwords, or other sensitive information into AI tools.
  • Interview notes and recordings require permission.
  • Testing should use realistic but non-sensitive examples.
  • Identifying details should be removed before sharing.
  • Families should receive clear information about any surveys, interviews, or focus groups.

How families and community members participate

Review communication

Comment on clarity, expectations, privacy, accommodations, and responsible AI.

Share learning experiences

Describe supports, barriers, routines, and questions without selecting the student solution.

Test a prototype

Provide feedback on clarity, usefulness, trust, and accessibility when appropriate.

Attend a showcase

Ask about evidence, learner agency, limitations, and next steps.

Give feedback

Help improve safety, value, logistics, and student support.

Raise concerns

Contact the project team about privacy, accessibility, participation, or events.

Accessibility and accommodations

Advisors should consider reading level, visual access, hearing access, motor access, language access, cognitive load, device availability, and low-tech alternatives. Families may share accommodation needs privately with the appropriate program contact.

Showcases and event logistics

Dates, location, virtual access, food, transportation, interpretation, accessibility, media permissions, and other logistics are TBD. Confirmed information should be shared in advance.

Feedback and contact

Families may be invited to complete a feedback survey about transparency, safety, accessibility, value, student support, and event experience. Contact information should be posted before public launch.
