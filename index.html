A human-centered learning-design program

Students investigate authentic learning challenges, study how people learn, design evidence-informed learning tools, test them with users, and improve them through feedback.

Starts with real learning problems

Students begin with needs identified by teachers and learners—not with an app idea.

Uses learning science

Teams identify the smallest meaningful component and connect it to evidence about learning.

Creates tools for other learners

Students generally create one shared team product rather than separate individual products.

Uses AI selectively

AI may support research, design, building, delivery, or critical study. The final tool may not use AI.

Preserves learner thinking

Tools should support productive struggle, explanation, reflection, metacognition, and self-regulation.

Improves through testing

Teams observe what users do, revise from evidence, and move backward when assumptions are wrong.

AI literacy is part of every project. Students examine human-versus-machine reasoning, prompting, output evaluation, privacy, bias, and responsible use even when the strongest final learning tool does not use AI.

The League Journey

One journey, two goals.

Students may design for their own learning or someone else’s. At the same time, they build their own understanding of how people learn and how AI can and cannot support learning.
