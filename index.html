Human-centered learning design

Students investigate real learning challenges—and design tools that help people learn.

The Student AI Learning Innovation League is a curriculum, club, showcase, and future competition model. Students listen to teachers and learners, study how people learn, create evidence-informed learning tools, test them with users, and improve them through feedback.

Core idea: AI should support productive struggle, reflection, metacognition, self-regulation, and deeper understanding—not replace the learner’s thinking.

What students do

Investigate authentic challenges

Students begin with problems identified by teacher-clients and peer learners, not with a platform or app idea.

Study human learning

Teams examine prior knowledge, memory, motivation, feedback, cognitive load, metacognition, self-regulation, transfer, and accessibility.

Design a learning tool

The tool may be paper-based, a game, flashcards, a graphic organizer, a learning activity, a prompt, a Gem, a chatbot, or a custom application.

Test with users

Students observe what people actually do—not only what they say they liked—and look for evidence of the target learning behavior.

Improve through evidence

Testing may send teams backward to redefine the problem, research a different strategy, or build a simpler prototype.

Share an educational justification

Teams explain the learning challenge, evidence, design choices, responsible use, impact, limitations, and lessons learned.

The role of AI

AI is one resource—not the purpose of the League

Research partner

Help generate search terms, summarize ideas, surface questions, and compare explanations. Students verify every important claim.

Design assistant

Support brainstorming, scenario generation, wording, mock interactions, and alternatives without selecting the educational solution.

Tool-building platform

Help create a Gem, chatbot, prototype, or custom application when conversational or generative behavior adds genuine value.

Delivery mechanism

Provide questions, graduated hints, feedback, role-play, retrieval practice, or reflection within a carefully designed interaction.

Critical object of study

Students examine hallucinations, bias, privacy, human-versus-machine reasoning, answer substitution, and responsible use.

Optional—not mandatory

The strongest solution may be a non-AI learning aid. Technical sophistication does not determine educational quality.

What makes the League distinctive
  • Students begin with real learning problems identified by teachers and peers.
  • Students use learning science to diagnose the smallest meaningful component.
  • Students create tools for other learners, not only for themselves.
  • The League combines classroom curriculum, clubs, showcases, and future competition.
  • The innovation is the League model—not a single AI platform.
  • The current MVP emphasizes one shared team product rather than one product per student.
