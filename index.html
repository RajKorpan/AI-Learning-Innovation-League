Learn how humans learn. Design AI that helps.

Students creating AI-supported tools for deeper STEM learning.

The Student AI Learning Innovation League helps young people understand human learning, build critical AI literacy, identify authentic learning challenges, and create responsible AI-supported learning aids for themselves and their peers.

See how the League worksLearn about the vision
The student design journey
  1. Understand human learning and AI
  2. Define a meaningful learning goal
  3. Diagnose a specific learning challenge
  4. Select an evidence-based learning strategy
  5. Design and build an AI-supported learning aid
  6. Test, revise, and share the result
Why this matters

AI should support learning—not replace it.

Many students encounter AI as a shortcut for getting answers or completing tasks. The League offers a different model: students become reflective learners, critical thinkers, and designers of tools that promote explanation, practice, feedback, and productive struggle.

1

Understand learning

Students explore motivation, memory, metacognition, practice, self-regulation, and the difference between human understanding and machine output.

2

Diagnose the challenge

Students identify what a learner is trying to do, break the task into components, and locate the specific knowledge, skill, or reasoning challenge.

3

Design responsibly

Students create AI interactions aligned with evidence-based learning strategies while considering accuracy, privacy, accessibility, and human oversight.

The initial model combines a five-lesson introduction with a student-led club process. Over time, clubs may connect through public showcases, cross-school events, shared resources, and competitions focused on learning science, responsible AI, and student innovation.

For advisors

Coach the process without taking it over.

Advisors guide the five lessons, support student teams, review safety and privacy, help students test ideas, and create an environment for collaboration and reflection.

For students

Build something that helps another learner think.

Students choose authentic STEM learning challenges and create tools that ask good questions, provide useful feedback, encourage reflection, and preserve learner agency.

What participation looks like

A complete learning and design experience

Students learn

Students develop a working understanding of memory, practice, feedback, metacognition, self-regulation, and the limits of AI-generated information. They examine when AI can strengthen thinking and when it can encourage dependence or cognitive offloading.

Students investigate

Each project begins with a real learner. Students gather evidence through interviews, observations, reflection, or work samples and identify the exact component of a STEM task that is difficult.

Students design

Teams select an evidence-based learning strategy and translate it into an AI interaction with clear roles, prompts, feedback rules, limits, and safety protections.

Students improve

Students test their prototype, observe where users struggle, check outputs for accuracy and fairness, revise the design, and explain what the tool still cannot do.
