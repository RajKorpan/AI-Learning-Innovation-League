Events and Showcases

Bring students, families, educators, and researchers together around the learning and design process.

Dates and formal League events are TBD. This page provides formats and expectations schools can use for local, virtual, cross-school, or Hunter College sharing.

Possible formats

Ways to organize a sharing event

School showcase

Teams present to classmates, teachers, administrators, and families.

Virtual showcase

Students from different schools share projects through short presentations, breakout feedback, and live demonstrations.

Cross-school design exchange

Teams test one another's tools, compare strategies, and suggest revisions.

Hunter College event

Students connect with faculty, staff, undergraduates, and researchers while learning about college pathways.

Family night

Families review student work, ask questions about safety and learning, and provide feedback.

Boot camp and showcase day

Students complete a focused design challenge, test prototypes, and present the same day.

What students should present

The full project story

  • Intended learner and learning goal
  • Specific challenge and evidence
  • Task analysis
  • Learning strategy and rationale
  • AI interaction and prototype
  • Safety, privacy, and accessibility safeguards
  • User-testing evidence
  • Revisions and remaining limitations
Interactive stations

Make the event more than a series of speeches

Project demonstration

Visitors try a short, non-sensitive interaction and give feedback.

Design notebook

Students show interview evidence, prompt versions, testing notes, and revision decisions.

Judge interview

Students answer questions about strategy alignment, safeguards, and limitations.

Peer critique

Teams give one strength, one question, and one evidence-based suggestion.

Family feedback

Families comment on clarity, usefulness, trust, and accessibility.

Reflection wall

Students share what they learned about AI, human learning, and collaboration.
