Club Roadmap

A flexible path from orientation to showcase.

Use this roadmap as a semester blueprint, then shorten or extend it based on your setting.

Semester roadmap

Twelve recommended phases

Phase 1
Orientation and recruitment
Introduce the League, review expectations, confirm technology, and form the student group.
Phase 2
Five introductory lessons
Build shared knowledge about learning science, AI literacy, responsible use, and the design process.
Phase 3
Learning-challenge discovery
Students interview peers, reflect on their own experiences, and identify authentic STEM learning needs.
Phase 4
Task analysis
Break the learning task into required knowledge, concepts, procedures, decisions, and reasoning.
Phase 5
Strategy selection
Match the challenge to an evidence-based learning strategy and justify the choice.
Phase 6
Prototype design
Define the AI role, interaction flow, feedback rules, constraints, and safety protections.
Phase 7
Build
Create a no-code tool, remix an existing example, or develop a reviewable storyboard or prompt prototype.
Phase 8
Safety review
Check privacy, accuracy, bias, accessibility, age restrictions, school policy, and human oversight.
Phase 9
User testing
Observe a peer or learner using the tool and collect structured feedback.
Phase 10
Revision
Use test evidence to improve the prompt, interaction flow, feedback, usability, or safeguards.
Phase 11
Showcase preparation
Prepare a demonstration, poster, design notebook, reflection, and explanation of limitations.
Phase 12
Share and reflect
Present to peers, teachers, families, or community guests and identify next steps.
Advisor checkpoints

Pause before students move forward

Challenge approval

Is the learning challenge specific, evidence-supported, and appropriate for the project?

Strategy approval

Does the selected learning strategy actually match the diagnosed need?

Safety approval

Are the platform, data, user-testing plan, and privacy protections acceptable?

Testing approval

Is the tester appropriate, informed, and protected from unnecessary data collection?

Revision checkpoint

Did students use evidence rather than preference alone to guide changes?

Showcase readiness

Can students explain the learning rationale, AI behavior, test evidence, changes, and limits?

Short-cycle option

Six-meeting roadmap

  1. Human learning, AI literacy, and project orientation
  2. Learning challenge and task analysis
  3. Learning strategy and AI interaction design
  4. Build and safety review
  5. User testing and revision
  6. Showcase and reflection
