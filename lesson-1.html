Five-Lesson Curriculum

Lesson 1: Humans Learn; AI Generates

Essential question: How is human learning different from what AI systems do?

Lesson 1 Lesson 2 Lesson 3 Lesson 4 Lesson 5
Learning goals
  • Explain that learning involves changes in knowledge, skills, strategies, and understanding.
  • Describe generative AI as a pattern-based system that produces outputs from data and instructions.
  • Distinguish productive AI support from cognitive offloading.
Suggested outputs
  • Opening comparison activity
  • Human-versus-AI concept map
  • Discussion of helpful and unhelpful uses
  • Exit reflection

Learning-science concepts

Prior knowledgeAttentionPracticeMemoryUnderstandingExpertise

AI-literacy concepts

Generative AITraining dataPattern predictionOutput evaluationCognitive offloading
Suggested lesson flow
  1. Launch the essential question with a concrete example.
  2. Introduce the core learning-science and AI-literacy ideas.
  3. Engage students in an active sort, model, interview, design task, or user test.
  4. Create a student artifact that becomes part of the final project.
  5. Use an exit reflection or advisor checkpoint to prepare for the next lesson.
Adaptation note: This lesson can be taught in a classroom, small group, virtual club, or one-on-one tutoring setting. Adjust examples, reading level, and discussion structure for the age group.
Discussion guide

Questions to deepen student thinking

  • Can an AI produce a correct answer without understanding it?
  • What changes inside a learner when learning occurs?
  • When does AI support practice, explanation, or reflection?
  • When does AI remove useful struggle?
  • What evidence would show that a learner—not only the AI—improved?

Common misconceptions

“AI knows everything.”

AI can produce plausible but incorrect outputs and does not have human experience or understanding.

“A correct answer means learning occurred.”

Learning requires remembering, explaining, applying, and transferring knowledge or skill.
