Five-Lesson Curriculum

Lesson 5: Test, Evaluate, Revise, and Share

Essential question: How do we know whether an AI learning aid is actually useful?

Lesson 1 Lesson 2 Lesson 3 Lesson 4 Lesson 5
Learning goals
  • Test the prototype with a peer or sample user.
  • Evaluate learning support, accuracy, usability, safety, and accessibility.
  • Revise the design using evidence and explain remaining limitations.
Suggested outputs
  • Think-aloud test
  • Observation notes
  • Revision log
  • Final presentation

Learning-science concepts

FeedbackReflectionRevisionTransferSelf-regulation

AI-literacy concepts

AI output evaluationBias checksResponsible sharingHuman accountability
Suggested lesson flow
  1. Launch the essential question with a concrete example.
  2. Introduce the core learning-science and AI-literacy ideas.
  3. Engage students in an active sort, model, interview, design task, or user test.
  4. Create a student artifact that becomes part of the final project.
  5. Use an exit reflection or advisor checkpoint to prepare for the next lesson.
Adaptation note: This lesson can be taught in a classroom, small group, virtual club, or one-on-one tutoring setting. Adjust examples, reading level, and discussion structure for the age group.
Evidence from testing

What students should record

  • What the user tried to do
  • Where the user became confused
  • Whether the AI followed the intended strategy
  • Whether it gave too much or too little support
  • Incorrect, unsafe, biased, or inaccessible outputs
  • What the team changed and why
  • What limitation still remains
Revision standard: A meaningful revision changes the learning interaction, feedback, usability, safety, or accuracy. Changing colors or wording alone is not enough unless it improves accessibility or understanding.
