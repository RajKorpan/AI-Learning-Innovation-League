Advisor Hub

Guide the entire League cycle without taking over student decisions.

Advisors coordinate people, permissions, teacher-client connections, schedules, technology, safety, family communication, team formation, curriculum, testing, evaluation, and showcase preparation.

Pilot readiness checklist

People and permissions

Advisor, students, teacher-client, peer learners, families, administrator or technology approvals, and any research permissions.

Technology options

Paper tools, approved AI assistants, Gems, no-code tools, or custom applications—with low-tech alternatives.

Interview scheduling

Practice, teacher-client, and peer learner interviews with permission and realistic time.

Team formation

One small team product, five simplified roles, rotation, accessible participation, and contribution records.

Implementation calendar

Five-week pilot, six-week, ten-week, semester, virtual, one-on-one, classroom, lunch, boot camp, or showcase-day model.

Safety and family communication

Privacy, accessibility, responsible AI, family guide, logistics, contact, and feedback procedures.

Advisor cycle

  1. Confirm readiness, permissions, format, schedule, and one team product.
  2. Connect students with a teacher-client and practice empathy interviews.
  3. Teach the five-lesson curriculum while students produce project artifacts.
  4. Review problem size, task analysis, evidence, and product-type decision.
  5. Approve the prototype, AI use, accessibility, and testing plan.
  6. Facilitate testing, evidence-based revision, reflection, and team conflict resolution.
  7. Use the rubric formatively and prepare students for a showcase level.
  8. Complete required evaluation responsibilities and document implementation lessons.

What advisors should not do

  • Select the solution for students.
  • Rewrite the student product.
  • Lead empathy interviews in place of students.
  • Overvalue technical complexity.
  • Allow AI to make unsupported educational decisions.
  • Treat the showcase as the only goal.
  • Permit identifiable data or unapproved tools.
  • Claim impact that the evidence does not support.

Troubleshooting

ProblemAdvisor response
Students jump to a chatbot idea.Return to empathy, problem size, and the “Does AI add value?” decision guide.
The teacher-client problem is too broad.Use task analysis to select one prerequisite, decision, representation, or mental action.
The team disagrees.Compare options using evidence, constraints, and the rubric rather than status or preference.
A student dominates the build.Rotate roles and require every student to explain the educational justification.
Testing produces weak evidence.Clarify the target behavior, use a realistic task, and compare what the learner said with what the learner did.
Technology fails.Use the paper, storyboard, role-play, or screenshot fallback.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
