Hunter College Project Team

An interdisciplinary team spanning AI, learning sciences, educational technology, computing education, and school implementation.

The Student AI Learning Innovation League is being developed by Hunter College researchers and technologists working with a New York City computer science educator and a parent–educator partner. Each team member contributes a distinct part of the planning, curriculum, club design, technical development, implementation, and evaluation work.

Hunter College research and technology team

Project leadership and core areas of expertise

RK

Raj Korpan

Principal Investigator · Hunter College Department of Computer Science · Director, Trustworthy, Intelligent, and Explainable Robotics Lab

Raj leads the project’s overall planning, coordination, AI systems work, computing-education components, and technical evaluation. He directs Hunter College’s Trustworthy, Intelligent, and Explainable Robotics (TIER) Lab, whose work sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, human–robot interaction, machine learning, robotics, cognitive science, and ethics.

His project contributions include developing the AI-literacy sequence, shaping student-facing activities about human versus machine reasoning, supporting the advisor-tool wireframe, identifying measures for AI literacy and responsible use, and evaluating whether student-designed tools are trustworthy, transparent, ethical, and aligned with their intended learning goals.

Primary project responsibilities: project leadership; AI systems and computing education; technical evaluation; advisor-tool usability; assessment and ethics; trustworthy and explainable AI connections.

RB

Rhonda Bondie

Co-Principal Investigator · Learning sciences, inclusive instructional design, and evaluation · Director, Hunter College Learning Lab

Rhonda leads the project’s learning-science foundation, inclusive instructional design, curriculum development, and evaluation planning. She directs the Hunter College Learning Lab (HCLL), the project’s primary setting for classroom and extracurricular pilot activities.

HCLL serves P–12 students with Individualized Education Programs through free, twice-weekly tutoring in reading, writing, mathematics, and personalized learning goals. Graduate teachers use individualized, evidence-based instructional practices under Hunter faculty supervision. This setting enables the team to examine whether the five lessons are understandable, age appropriate, accessible, and feasible in authentic teaching contexts.

Primary project responsibilities: human-learning curriculum; metacognition and self-regulation content; inclusive lesson design; teacher implementation supports; student pre/post measures; evaluation planning; HCLL pilot coordination; family and teacher feedback.

KH

Kenny Hirschmann

Co-Principal Investigator and Technologist · Manager, Frankfort Center for Learning and Scholarly Technologies

Kenny contributes instructional design, educational technology, teacher supports, implementation planning, and technical development. He manages the Frankfort Center for Learning and Scholarly Technologies in the Hunter College School of Education.

The Frankfort Center supports the exploration and integration of technology across curriculum, teaching, and research. Its services include customized pedagogical strategies, technology-enriched course design, implementation and evaluation support, workshops, consultations, and access to instructional technologies and collaborative spaces.

For the League, Kenny helps translate the project’s learning-science and AI goals into clear lesson structures, detailed facilitation guides, advisor workflows, and a usable website or wireframe that teachers can navigate without extensive technical expertise.

Primary project responsibilities: instructional design; educational technology; teacher and advisor supports; curriculum implementation guidance; wireframe and interface design; technical planning; usability and accessibility.

ET

Edgar Troudt

Co-Principal Investigator, Technologist, and Entrepreneur Lead · Director of Programs in Computer Science Education

Edgar directs Hunter College’s programs in Computer Science Education in the School of Education’s Department of Curriculum and Teaching, including the Computer Science Education MSEd and Advanced Certificate programs.

His work creates a direct bridge among AI literacy, computer science teacher preparation, ethics and computing, curriculum design, and clinical experiences in P–12 settings. He contributes expertise in AI for learning, computer science education, Lean Launch methods, club design, and long-term sustainability.

Within the project, Edgar helps define the AI Learning Innovation Club model, student activity sequence, advisor requirements, and future scale-up strategy. He also supports the design of student activities in which learners examine, remix, and improve existing AI tools so that they are more clearly grounded in learning theory.

Primary project responsibilities: club operating model; AI for learning; computer science education; student tool-building processes; Lean Launch and entrepreneurship; sustainability; professional-learning and teacher-education pathways.

School and family partners

Implementation and stakeholder expertise

LC

Luis Collado

K–12 Educator Partner · Brooklyn Technical High School

Luis is a New York State–certified computer science teacher and a programming and electrical mentor and coach for Brooklyn Technical High School’s FIRST Robotics Competition team, Team 334.

He contributes direct knowledge of secondary computer science classrooms, student motivation, robotics-team structures, mentoring, technical projects, and afterschool implementation. His experience helps the team judge whether the curriculum and club expectations are realistic for students and manageable for school-based advisors.

Primary project responsibilities: classroom and club implementation; educator feedback; robotics and competition-model expertise; student engagement; feasibility review; support for curriculum and club pilots.

KH

Khalya Hopkins, Ed.D.

Parent and Guardian Partner · Educator and Researcher

Khalya contributes the parent and caregiver perspective to the project. She is the parent of a child in P–12 education and an adult child in higher education and the workforce, giving her a view of how AI is affecting learners at different stages.

She is also an educator and researcher of teaching practices that promote achievement for all students. Her participation helps the team examine whether the League is transparent, understandable, supportive of student learning, responsive to family concerns, and attentive to the rapidly changing role of AI in education and work.

Primary project responsibilities: family perspective; student support; achievement-oriented teaching practices; family communication; transparency; accessibility and trust; feedback on community value and participation.

Hunter College resources

Institutional settings that support the project

Hunter College Learning Lab

Provides a structured P–12 tutoring setting for piloting the curriculum, observing implementation, gathering student and teacher feedback, and engaging families.

Frankfort Center

Provides educational technology expertise, instructional spaces, devices, workshops, consultation, and support for accessible implementation.

Computer Science Education Programs

Connect the League to teacher preparation, curriculum design, ethics and computing, clinical practice, and future professional learning.

Department of Computer Science

Contributes expertise and infrastructure in AI, machine learning, robotics, explainable AI, security, privacy, software systems, and computing education.

TIER Lab

Supports trustworthy and explainable AI, human-centered technology development, participatory design, and critical evaluation of AI-supported learning tools.

School and Community Partnerships

Connect the planning work to real classrooms, afterschool clubs, robotics mentoring, families, and future cross-school implementation.

Why this team composition matters: The League is not solely a technology project or solely a curriculum project. It requires expertise in human learning, AI systems, teacher preparation, educational technology, school implementation, family engagement, ethics, accessibility, and sustainability.
