Evaluation

Learn what worked, for whom, under what conditions, and what should change.

Evaluation supports program improvement. Educational participation, research participation, surveys, focus groups, interviews, and public sharing should be explained clearly and handled according to required permissions and approved procedures.

Evidence sources

Student artifacts

Empathy notes, problem definitions, task maps, evidence records, prototypes, testing logs, revisions, and presentations.

Student feedback

Experience, confidence, understanding, collaboration, responsible AI, and perceived value.

Teacher and advisor feedback

Clarity, feasibility, time, technology, safety, accessibility, student engagement, and revisions.

Family feedback

Transparency, privacy, support, accommodations, value, event experience, and trust.

Usability evidence

Whether users can find and apply the website guidance without excessive explanation.

Implementation record

Attendance, timing, deviations, barriers, supports, and contextual conditions.

Advisor responsibilities

  • Use the current approved instruments and procedures.
  • Explain which activities are part of the club and which are research or evaluation.
  • Follow consent, assent, permission, privacy, and data-handling requirements.
  • Do not promise confidentiality or benefits beyond approved language.
  • Record implementation changes and barriers.
  • Report concerns to the project team.
  • Do not upload identifiable evaluation data to AI tools.

Interpret small-pilot findings carefully

A planning pilot can show feasibility, usability, completion, patterns, and promising evidence. It should not be described as proving broad impact or effectiveness.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
