Project Examples

Examples demonstrate possibilities—not answers to copy.

Every team must collect its own empathy evidence, define a specific component, verify research, compare product types, and test with appropriate users.

AI-assisted non-AI tools

Graph scale card set

AI helps draft varied graph examples; students verify them and create physical cards that practice intervals and scale.

Test-question breakdown routine

A paper checklist helps learners identify action words, information, constraints, and steps.

Vocabulary memory game

AI drafts candidate examples; students curate a game using retrieval, spacing, and elaboration.

Reading-comprehension organizer

A graphic organizer helps learners identify evidence, inference, and confidence.

Gems and chatbots

Question-decomposition coach

Asks the learner to identify what is being asked, what information matters, and what step comes first.

Error-analysis coach

Compares the learner’s reasoning with an example and uses graduated hints.

Debate partner

Challenges claims, asks for evidence, and prompts reflection without writing the final response.

Reflection coach

Prompts planning, monitoring, strategy choice, confidence with evidence, and next steps.

Advanced or community-sensitive examples

Custom simulation

An application lets learners make a decision, observe a consequence, explain reasoning, and revise.

AI and endangered or Indigenous languages

Projects may explore language learning or documentation only with community leadership, consent, cultural and data governance, appropriate expertise, and clear limits. A technically possible tool may still be inappropriate.

Learning-progress mapper

A tool helps learners see prerequisites, current performance, next steps, and connections to advanced work.

Example review: What is the learner action? What evidence supports the strategy? What does AI add? What risk or access issue should be tested? Could a simpler tool work better?
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
