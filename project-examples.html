Project Examples

Examples of learning-science-based AI tools.

These examples are starting points. Students should adapt the design to a real learner and test whether it actually helps.

Retrieval Practice Coach

Challenge: remembering biology vocabulary.
Strategy: retrieval and spacing.
AI behavior: ask for recall, revisit missed ideas, and give brief corrective feedback.

Scientific Explanation Coach

Challenge: knowing facts but not connecting cause and effect.
Strategy: elaboration and self-explanation.
AI behavior: ask why and how, then identify missing links.

Math Error Analyzer

Challenge: repeating the same algebra mistake.
Strategy: error analysis and feedback.
AI behavior: compare steps and ask where reasoning changed.

Task Decomposition Guide

Challenge: not knowing how to start a complex task.
Strategy: chunking and planning.
AI behavior: break the task into steps and ask the learner to choose a starting point.

Data Storytelling Robot

Challenge: interpreting data and communicating patterns.
Strategy: visual-verbal representation and explanation.
AI behavior: guide students in turning data into a narrative and checking claims.

Metacognitive Reflection Partner

Challenge: difficulty monitoring progress.
Strategy: reflection and self-regulation.
AI behavior: ask about goals, confidence, strategy use, and next steps.

How to use examples

Examples are models, not answers

Students should examine what makes each example strong, identify what information is still missing, and redesign the idea for a specific learner. Teams should not simply copy a prompt or tool without collecting their own evidence and testing the result.

For each example, ask: What is the learning goal? What is the challenge? What strategy is used? What does the learner still have to do? What should be tested?
