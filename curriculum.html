Five-Lesson Curriculum

The curriculum gives students a shared foundation in human learning, AI literacy, empathy, problem definition, research, prototyping, testing, reflection, and educational justification.

Lesson 1: Understand Human Learning and AI

Students will

  • Explain why an answer is not the same as learning.
  • Describe productive struggle, metacognition, self-regulation, prior knowledge, feedback, and transfer.
  • Identify five roles AI may play.
  • Recognize when a non-AI tool may be better.
  • Reject fixed learning-style labels as a design basis.

Suggested activities

  • Compare a learner solving a problem with an AI generating an answer.
  • Sort examples into support, substitution, research, design, delivery, and critical study.
  • Rewrite a learning myth as an evidence-based question.
  • Create team principles for AI that supports learning.

Project artifact

Human-learning and AI design principles.

Lesson 2: Listen Through Empathy Interviews

Students will

  • Explain why interviews come before solution design.
  • Use starter questions flexibly and ask follow-ups.
  • Listen for stories, emotions, needs, strategies, and workarounds.
  • Synthesize evidence without leading the interviewee.

Suggested activities

  • Practice with a teammate or chatbot role-play.
  • Prepare or conduct a teacher-client interview.
  • Prepare or conduct a peer learner interview.
  • Create insight statements and list changed assumptions.

Project artifact

Empathy interview notes and synthesized insights.

Lesson 3: Define and Research the Problem

Students will

  • Use the Problem Size Check.
  • Break a task into knowledge, actions, prerequisites, decisions, errors, and cognitive-load points.
  • Select the smallest meaningful component.
  • Evaluate evidence strength and identify learning myths.
  • Map the component in a larger progression.

Suggested activities

  • Revise a broad problem into a precise one.
  • Complete the Task Analysis tool.
  • Add at least three evidence records.
  • Create beginning, developing, and proficient performance descriptions.

Project artifact

Defined problem, task map, progression, and evidence record.

Lesson 4: Ideate and Prototype

Students will

  • Generate several learning-tool concepts.
  • Decide whether AI adds value.
  • Select a product type based on learner action and evidence.
  • Create a low-resolution prototype that answers one question.
  • Apply privacy, accessibility, agency, and fading checks.

Suggested activities

  • Compare paper, activity, prompt, Gem, and application concepts.
  • Use the Prompt Builder when conversational AI fits.
  • Create a storyboard, role-play, paper flow, sample set, or wireframe.
  • Write the prototype question and test plan.

Project artifact

Concept comparison and low-resolution prototype.

Lesson 5: Test, Improve, Reflect, and Share

Students will

  • Use staged testing and realistic tasks.
  • Record behavior, statements, learning evidence, and unexpected outcomes.
  • Revise from evidence and consider redefining the problem.
  • Complete reflection, rubric, and educational justification.
  • Present accessibly and protect privacy.

Suggested activities

  • Conduct team self-test and peer test.
  • Prepare teacher-client and learner tests.
  • Complete a revision log and retest.
  • Create the final presentation and reflection.

Project artifact

Testing record, revision, educational justification, and presentation.

Adapt the curriculum without losing the core

  • Use age-appropriate examples and reading levels.
  • Offer paper, digital, oral, visual, and assisted formats.
  • Keep empathy, task analysis, evidence, and testing even in short cycles.
  • Reduce project scope rather than removing student ownership.
  • Connect each lesson to the shared team product.
  • Use the Project Studio to save and download student work.
