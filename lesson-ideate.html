Facilitation Guide

Mission

Help students choose a learning move that fits the problem, then create several possible designs using resources they actually have.

Key questionWhat should the learner actually do to practice the part that is difficult?
Use this guide when you need it

Prepare, then facilitate

Before the sessionStep 1
Step 1

Prepare

You do not need to reteach the student activity here. Prepare the conditions that let students do the thinking themselves.

Before students arrive

  • Have the Define learning goal and success indicator available.
  • Preview the Strategy Warm-up and Strategy Explorer.
  • Gather visible materials students can build with: paper, cards, markers, classroom objects, devices, and people.
  • Be ready to remind students that a strategy is what the learner does; a product is what helps them do it.
Step 2

Know what to watch for

Watch for

  • Students choosing a chatbot before deciding what learning action is needed.
  • Treating a Strategy Explorer result as the one correct answer.
  • Generating one idea and defending it immediately.
  • Ignoring physical, social, or low-tech resources already available.

If students get stuck, try saying

  • “What do you want the learner to do more successfully: remember, explain, compare, apply, check, or practice over time?”
  • “What could make that action happen without using a screen?”
  • “Give me three very different ideas before choosing one.”
Step 3

Preview the student path

The student pathway contains the actual activity, examples, tools, and response scaffolds. Open it before the session so you know what students will see.

Step 1

Choose the session length

Both versions follow the same student journey. The expanded option gives students more time to think, build, discuss, or revise.

Step 2

Activities

Use these prompts to facilitate. The actual student instructions and tools stay on the student page.

0–7

Try learning moves

Run the Strategy Warm-up. Let students choose before revealing the learning-science name.

Open the student activity ↗

Students produce: experience with several learning moves

7–17

Match a strategy

Students use Strategy Explorer to get several strategy hypotheses. Ask them to focus on the learner action, not the formal term.

Open the student activity ↗

Students produce: a selected strategy hypothesis

17–22

Try the chosen strategy

Students complete the two-minute mini-activity for the strategy they selected, then notice what the learner actually has to do.

Open the student activity ↗

Students produce: a concrete understanding of the selected strategy

22–32

Use what you already have

Students identify materials, people, spaces, interests, time, and access needs that could shape the design.

Open the student activity ↗

Students produce: a resource inventory and several possible directions

32–40

Make several ideas

Require several different ideas before shortlisting. Encourage at least one off-screen option.

Open the student activity ↗

Students produce: multiple design ideas and a shortlist

40–45

Check together

Ask students to justify the shortlist using the learning goal and strategy rather than novelty or technical complexity.

Open the student activity ↗

Students produce: a facilitator-checked design direction

Step 3

What students should produce

Before moving on, students should have these artifacts or decisions. They do not need polished final work.

Session outputs

  • Selected learning-strategy hypothesis
  • What the learner should actually do
  • What success should look like
  • Resource inventory
  • Several different design ideas
  • One or two shortlisted ideas
Step 4

Quick check

Use these questions to decide whether students are ready to move forward or need one more conversation/revision.

Check before moving on

  • Can students explain the strategy without relying on jargon?
  • Does the idea make the learner do meaningful thinking?
  • Did the team consider off-screen/low-tech options?
  • Are students choosing based on fit rather than technical impressiveness?
