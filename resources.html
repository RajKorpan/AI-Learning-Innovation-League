Core topics

Human learning

Changes in knowledge, mental models, strategies, fluency, self-monitoring, and transfer.

Memory

Encoding, retrieval, spacing, interference, prior knowledge, and durable access.

Motivation

Agency, relevance, belonging, expectancy, value, progress, and persistence.

Metacognition

Planning, monitoring, identifying confusion, evaluating strategies, and reflecting.

Self-regulation

Goals, attention, time, motivation, help-seeking, and adaptive strategy use.

Productive struggle

Supported effort that keeps the learner thinking without creating unnecessary frustration.

Learning myths

Fixed visual, auditory, and kinesthetic learning-style matching is not an established basis for instruction.

Cognitive load

Task complexity, working-memory demands, examples, chunking, representations, and expertise.

Transfer

Flexible application across examples, contexts, representations, and disciplines.

Use evidence responsibly

  • Read beyond an AI summary.
  • Check whether the source is credible and relevant.
  • Distinguish a general principle from a guarantee for one learner.
  • Record mixed or limited evidence.
  • Translate a claim into a testable design implication.

Core areas

Human versus machine reasoning

Fluent generation, pattern prediction, understanding, experience, judgment, and accountability.

Prompting and instructions

Role, task, context, format, interaction sequence, examples, feedback, limits, and verification.

Output evaluation

Accuracy, evidence, uncertainty, relevance, bias, consistency, and educational alignment.

Privacy and data

Minimization, account requirements, retention, sharing, sensitive information, and consent.

Bias and fairness

Representation, stereotypes, unequal feedback, edge cases, and impacted users.

Responsible use

Learner agency, answer substitution, productive struggle, human review, accessibility, and escalation.

Project examples

AI-assisted paper tool

AI helps draft graph examples; students verify them and create physical practice cards.

Question-decomposition guide

A paper checklist or Gem helps learners identify what a question asks, what information matters, and what comes first.

Error-analysis coach

A conversational tool asks for reasoning, identifies one gap, and offers graduated hints.

Reading-comprehension organizer

A graphic organizer supports evidence, inference, monitoring, and reflection.

Vocabulary memory game

A curated game uses retrieval, spacing, and elaboration.

Community-sensitive language project

Language work requires community leadership, consent, cultural and data governance, expertise, and clear limits.

AI-assisted non-AI tools

Graph-scale card set

AI helps draft varied graph examples; students verify them and create physical cards that practice intervals and scale.

Test-question breakdown routine

A paper checklist helps learners identify action words, information, constraints, and steps.

Vocabulary memory game

AI drafts candidate examples; students curate a game using retrieval, spacing, and elaboration.

Reading-comprehension organizer

A graphic organizer helps learners identify evidence, inference, and confidence.

Gems and chatbots

Question-decomposition coach

Asks the learner to identify what is being asked, what information matters, and what step comes first.

Error-analysis coach

Compares the learner’s reasoning with an example and uses graduated hints.

Debate partner

Challenges claims, asks for evidence, and prompts reflection without writing the final response.

Reflection coach

Prompts planning, monitoring, strategy choice, confidence with evidence, and next steps.

Advanced or community-sensitive examples

Custom simulation

An application lets learners make a decision, observe a consequence, explain reasoning, and revise.

AI and endangered or Indigenous languages

Projects may explore language learning or documentation only with community leadership, consent, cultural and data governance, appropriate expertise, and clear limits. A technically possible tool may still be inappropriate.

Learning-progress mapper

A tool helps learners see prerequisites, current performance, next steps, and connections to advanced work.

Example review: What is the learner action? What evidence supports the strategy? What does AI add? What risk or access issue should be tested? Could a simpler tool work better?

What the League judges differently

  • Empathy and user understanding
  • Smallest meaningful component
  • Learning-science evidence
  • Alignment between need and design
  • Productive struggle and learner agency
  • Testing behavior, not only satisfaction
  • Accessibility and responsible AI
  • Educational justification and team reflection
