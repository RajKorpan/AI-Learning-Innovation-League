Learning Strategy Library

Choose the strategy that matches the challenge.

A strong AI learning aid is designed around how learning should happen—not around what the technology can generate.

Retrieval practice

Use when: learners need to remember information.
AI role: ask for recall before revealing answers and revisit missed ideas.

Elaboration

Use when: learners know facts but cannot explain connections.
AI role: ask why, how, for examples, and for comparisons.

Worked examples and fading

Use when: learners do not understand a process.
AI role: model, then gradually remove support.

Feedback and error analysis

Use when: the learner repeats a particular mistake.
AI role: identify where reasoning changed and offer a targeted hint.

Chunking and task decomposition

Use when: a task has too many parts.
AI role: break the task into manageable steps and track progress.

Visual and verbal representation

Use when: relationships are difficult to organize.
AI role: prompt diagrams, labels, comparisons, and explanation of visuals.

Self-explanation

Use when: learners can perform steps but cannot explain why.
AI role: ask the learner to justify each step.

Metacognitive reflection

Use when: learners struggle to plan or monitor.
AI role: ask about goals, strategies, confusion, confidence, and next steps.

Spaced practice

Use when: learning fades over time.
AI role: schedule repeated practice and vary examples across sessions.

Design rule: Students should be able to complete the sentence, “My AI tool uses the strategy ___ by asking the learner to ___.”
Strategy selection cautions

A strategy can be misused

Retrieval without feedback

Repeatedly practicing an incorrect response can strengthen the error. Learners need timely correction.

Worked examples without fading

If support never decreases, learners may not develop independent skill.

Elaboration without focus

Too many open-ended questions can overwhelm a learner. Prompts should target the diagnosed challenge.

Metacognition without action

Reflection should lead to a strategy choice or next step, not only a confidence rating.
