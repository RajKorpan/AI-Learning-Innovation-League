Responsible AI, Safety, Privacy, and Accessibility

Design tools that protect learners, preserve human judgment, and work for varied users.

Every student project should address privacy, accuracy, bias, age appropriateness, school policy, accessibility, and human oversight from the beginning.

Responsible AI

Core design requirements

Protect personal information

Do not request names, addresses, schedules, medical information, disability status, account credentials, or private information about another person unless an approved activity clearly requires it.

Check accuracy

AI may produce false or misleading information. Require verification for important claims and make uncertainty visible.

Review bias and fairness

Test whether language, examples, assumptions, or feedback disadvantage or stereotype learners.

Follow school policy

Review acceptable-use policies, platform age restrictions, account rules, sharing settings, and data-retention practices.

Keep humans responsible

AI should not make high-stakes decisions or replace teachers, caregivers, counselors, or qualified professionals.

Preserve learner agency

The AI should ask, guide, model, or give feedback without completing the learner's work.

Accessibility and inclusion

Design for varied learners and technologies

Reading and language

Use plain language, define terms, provide examples, and support multilingual learners.

Visual access

Use readable contrast, scalable text, descriptive labels, and information that does not depend on color alone.

Keyboard and assistive technology

Ensure controls can be reached by keyboard and content is compatible with screen readers where possible.

Cognitive accessibility

Break tasks into steps, reduce unnecessary complexity, show progress, and use predictable interaction patterns.

Participation options

Offer speaking, writing, drawing, building, testing, documenting, and presenting roles.

Technology alternatives

Provide low-bandwidth, shared-device, printed, storyboard, and advisor-mediated options.

Pre-test review

Questions teams must answer before involving a user

  1. What data does the tool use or request?
  2. Could it reveal or infer private information?
  3. How will important outputs be verified?
  4. What harmful, biased, or inappropriate response could occur?
  5. What should the AI refuse to do?
  6. What must an advisor monitor?
  7. Can users navigate and understand the interaction?
  8. Is an alternative format available?
  9. Is permission needed before testing?
Risk scenarios

What should the team do?

The AI asks for a full name and school

Stop, remove the request, and redesign the prompt so only necessary learning information is used.

The AI gives a confident false explanation

Document the error, add verification rules, and require a trusted source or adult review.

The AI gives unequal feedback

Test more examples and review language, assumptions, and inconsistent patterns.

A learner reveals sensitive information

Do not continue the interaction. Follow school procedures and involve an appropriate trusted adult.

The tool completes the assignment

Revise the interaction so it gives questions, hints, examples, or feedback while preserving learner responsibility.

The platform is inaccessible or not approved

Use a storyboard, paper prototype, advisor-controlled demonstration, or another approved option.

Accessibility testing questions

  • Could the user understand the instructions without extra explanation?
  • Could the user navigate using the available input method?
  • Was the language appropriate for the learner?
  • Did feedback remain respectful and understandable?
  • Could the same goal be reached in another format?
  • What barrier appeared, and how could the design reduce it?
