Responsible AI, Safety, and Privacy

Design tools that protect learners and preserve human judgment.

Every student project should address privacy, accuracy, bias, accessibility, age appropriateness, school policy, and human oversight.

Protect personal information

Do not request full names, addresses, schedules, medical information, disability status, account credentials, or private information about another person.

Check accuracy

AI may produce false or misleading information. Require verification for important claims and make uncertainty visible.

Review bias and fairness

Test whether language, examples, assumptions, or feedback disadvantage or stereotype learners.

Support accessibility

Use clear language, flexible formats, readable layouts, and alternatives for different learning and technology needs.

Follow policy

Review school acceptable-use policies, platform age restrictions, account rules, and data-retention practices.

Keep humans responsible

AI should not make high-stakes decisions or replace teachers, caregivers, counselors, or qualified professionals.

Safety review

Before user testing, answer these questions

  1. What data does the tool use or request?
  2. Could the tool reveal or infer private information?
  3. How will students verify important outputs?
  4. What harmful, biased, or inappropriate response could occur?
  5. What should the AI refuse to do?
  6. What must an advisor monitor?
  7. Is permission needed before testing?
Risk scenarios

What should the team do?

The AI asks for a full name and school

Stop, remove the request, and redesign the prompt so the tool uses only the information needed for learning.

The AI gives a confident false explanation

Document the error, add verification rules, and require a trusted source or adult review for important claims.

The AI gives different quality feedback to different learners

Test more examples, review language and assumptions, and look for inconsistent or biased patterns.

A learner reveals sensitive information

Do not continue the AI interaction. Follow school procedures and involve an appropriate trusted adult.

The tool completes the assignment

Revise the interaction so it gives questions, hints, examples, or feedback while preserving learner responsibility.

The platform is not approved

Use a storyboard, paper prototype, advisor-controlled demonstration, or another approved option.
