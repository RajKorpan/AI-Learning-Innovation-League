For teachers and facilitators

Facilitate a Learning League team

Help students turn a real STEM learning challenge into something small they can build, test, improve, and share.

What is it?A club-style design challenge

Students investigate how learning works and use AI thoughtfully while making something useful.

What will I do?Guide the process

Ask questions, keep the project focused, and help students test their ideas without taking over.

What will students do?Make and test a real solution

They may build a game, activity, printable tool, AI learning tool, physical experience, or another small prototype.

How much time?Five guided sessions

Use one 45-minute meeting per session or two 45-minute meetings when your group needs more time.

1 · Why join

Students learn by solving a real learning problem

The League feels more like robotics, debate, or a design club than another class. Students learn interviewing, learning science, AI literacy, prototyping, and testing because they need those skills to make something useful.

  • Work from a real STEM learning challenge.
  • Make something early and improve it through evidence.
  • Use AI when it adds value—not because AI is the goal.
  • Share what the team discovered, built, and changed.
Your role

Guide the team without taking over

What you do

  • Set up the time, space, and basic materials.
  • Ask questions that help students think.
  • Help students make an idea smaller when it is too broad.
  • Guide students through the League process.
  • Help students test, notice what happened, and improve.

What to avoid

  • Choosing the solution for students.
  • Doing the interviews for them.
  • Building the product for them.
  • Giving an answer when a question could move the thinking forward.
  • Assuming a more technical project is automatically better.

You do not need to be an AI or learning-science expert. Each session gives you the explanation, example, script, and student activity you need at that moment.

Step 1 of 6 · Confirm readiness

Step 1 · Confirm readiness

Confirm the basics before students begin

  • Facilitator identified
  • Meeting option plausible
  • School/platform requirements checked
  • Low-tech backup available
  • Support contact known
Why these basics matter

Confirm school requirements, space or virtual access, approved technology, and a paper/offline alternative before students depend on them.

Think about reading, language, visual, hearing, motor, attention, device, transportation, or scheduling needs without asking students to disclose more than is necessary. Know whom to contact when additional support is needed.

Start small: one facilitator, a small student team, one focused learning problem, and one low-resolution prototype are enough for a complete League cycle.

Step 3 · Recruit students and connect people

Know who is participating and whose learning challenge the project will address

Recruit students

  • Explain that teams can design something to help themselves learn or to help someone else learn.
  • Emphasize that students will learn about human learning and AI while they design; they do not need prior coding or AI expertise.
  • Recruit for curiosity and willingness to test ideas, not only technical skill.

Decide which teacher students will interview

You can be the teacher students interview. You do not need to recruit a separate teacher. The teacher should describe a real STEM learning challenge, what they observe students doing, and what successful performance would look like—without choosing the solution for the team.

  • If students interview you, bring one recent learning challenge from your own teaching.
  • If students interview another teacher, help arrange a short conversation and ask for a concrete example.
  • Keep the conversation focused on the learning problem rather than a requested product.

Communicate with families

When you recruit students, share the League purpose, how AI may be used, privacy expectations, access supports, and how student work may be shared.

Step 4 · Form and facilitate the team

Create a routine that keeps every student involved

Use five flexible roles

RoleMain responsibility
ListenInterviews, empathy, permission, and synthesizing learner needs.
LearningTask analysis, learning ideas, evidence, and checking assumptions.
DesignIdeas, product comparison, prototypes, and version changes.
TestTest questions, observation, learning evidence, and revision.
ShareDocumentation, project story, demonstration, and accessible presentation.

Roles should rotate or overlap. Every student should be able to explain the whole project.

Use the same meeting routine

  1. Review the current design stage and the evidence needed next.
  2. Assign or rotate roles.
  3. Complete one focused task.
  4. Pause for learning-goal, safety, and accessibility checks built into that session.
  5. Give one strength, one question, and one evidence-based critique.
  6. Record the decision and next action.

Keep participation inclusive

  • Offer oral, written, visual, asynchronous, and assisted ways to contribute.
  • Separate technical building from educational decision-making.
  • Rotate high-status tasks such as building and presenting.
  • Use clear chunks, models, checklists, and flexible pacing.
  • Reduce project scope instead of excluding a student from meaningful work.
  • Critique the design, not the person.
Step 5 · Select a schedule

Step 6 · Review and generate

Create your customized implementation plan

Generate a practical setup plan based on the choices you made. The plan will recommend team structure, facilitation supports, technology and access considerations, and direct links to the five facilitator sessions.

Before using the plan:

Review it against school requirements and confirm any still-unknown needs with students and families. The generated plan does not replace local accessibility, safety, privacy, or platform review.

3 · Once your team is running

The sessions are a scaffold, not a rigid course. Give more support when students are new to the process, then let them take more control as they are ready.
