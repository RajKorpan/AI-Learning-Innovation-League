Your League Journey

Your challenge is to design something that helps you or someone else learn—and to become a better investigator of how people learn and how AI can and cannot support learning. You do not need to know what you will build yet. Move through six missions, earn one useful project artifact at each stage, and make the important decisions with evidence.

Your project map

Move through six stages

0 of 6 completeStart with Empathize

Each stage is a mission. Complete the work, save the artifact, and use an advisor checkpoint when the decision needs another perspective. The process is iterative—you can return to an earlier stage whenever testing changes what you understand.

1EmpathizeUnderstand the ChallengeListen before you solve. Learn what the learner experiences, what the teacher notices, and what matters to them.You leave with: Empathy insightsNot started2DefineFind the BreakdownTurn a broad concern into a specific learning breakdown that one project can address.You leave with: Focused learning challengeNot started3IdeateInvent PossibilitiesExplore learning strategies, use resources already around you, and create several very different ideas.You leave with: A short list of promising ideasNot started4PrototypeBuild Something SmallChoose one idea and build the smallest version that can teach you whether the learning interaction works.You leave with: Testable prototypeNot started5TestPut It to the TestWatch someone use the prototype, collect evidence, and revise based on what actually happens.You leave with: Testing evidence and revisionNot started6ShareTell the StoryPrepare to explain the challenge, your reasoning, your tool, what changed through testing, and what you would improve next.You leave with: Sharing-event story and demoNot started
This is progress, not a ranking. Moving forward means your project has enough evidence to make the next decision. A simple, well-tested project is stronger than a complicated project that does not fit the learner.
Every stage has two kinds of learning.

Project learning: what helps this learner? League learning: what did your team learn about human learning and AI while making the decision?

Who are you trying to help learn?

League projects can start with a challenge you experience yourself or a challenge experienced by someone else. Either way, use evidence rather than assumptions.

Choose how much guidance you want

Your team can use more support at the beginning and less support as you become more independent. This is separate from how technically complicated your final product is.

What could a League project look like?

Three projects, three very different solutions

These examples show possibilities, not topics to copy. Notice that the design process—not the technology—determines the solution.

Mostly physical / non-AI

Slope and steepness

Starting problem: “I know the slope formula, but I don't understand what slope means.”

Possible direction: A ruler-and-whiteboard activity that connects rise/run to visible steepness before calculating.

Follow this example through the journey →
AI-assisted, non-AI product

Biology vocabulary

Starting problem: “I reread the words, but I can't remember them later.”

Possible direction: AI helps create and vary retrieval questions; students turn the best ones into a card challenge.

This can also be a self-design project: study your own learning, then ask another person to help you test your assumptions.

Follow this example through the journey →
Conversational AI

Understanding word problems

Starting problem: “I don't know what the question is asking me to do.”

Possible direction: A guided AI conversation that asks the learner to identify givens, unknowns, and relationships without solving the problem.

Before you begin

Work as one team

Roles help divide responsibility, but they are not permanent jobs. Rotate, overlap, and make sure everyone can explain the whole project.
