Learning Science Resources

Evidence about how people develop knowledge, skill, understanding, and self-direction.

Use these topics to ask better questions, not to apply a strategy automatically. Evidence must be connected to the target component, learner, task, and context.

Core topics

Human learning

Changes in knowledge, mental models, strategies, fluency, self-monitoring, and transfer.

Memory

Encoding, retrieval, spacing, interference, prior knowledge, and durable access.

Motivation

Agency, relevance, belonging, expectancy, value, progress, and persistence.

Metacognition

Planning, monitoring, identifying confusion, evaluating strategies, and reflecting.

Self-regulation

Goals, attention, time, motivation, help-seeking, and adaptive strategy use.

Productive struggle

Supported effort that keeps the learner thinking without creating unnecessary frustration.

Learning myths

Fixed visual, auditory, and kinesthetic learning-style matching is not an established basis for instruction.

Cognitive load

Task complexity, working-memory demands, examples, chunking, representations, and expertise.

Transfer

Flexible application across examples, contexts, representations, and disciplines.
