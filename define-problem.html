Define the Problem

Turn stories into a precise, testable learning challenge.

A usable problem is specific enough to connect to learning science, small enough to address in a short intervention, and observable enough to test.

Teacher-client problem examples

Reading comprehension

Locating the sentence or idea that answers an inference question.

Test-question breakdown

Identifying the action requested by a multi-part question.

Data-table interpretation

Locating the correct row, column, unit, and comparison.

Measurement conversion

Choosing a conversion relationship and checking whether the result is reasonable.

Graphing results

Selecting a scale and determining the interval between labeled points.

Division in science

Recognizing when a scientific quantity represents a ratio or rate.

Proportional reasoning

Distinguishing additive from multiplicative relationships.

Background knowledge

Retrieving a prerequisite fact or concept needed to interpret new information.

Vocabulary and facts

Recalling disciplinary terms and using them accurately in context.

Complex tasks

Planning, sequencing, monitoring, and checking a task with several steps.

From broad to precise

Too broad

“Students are bad at science.”

Better

“Students struggle to interpret the axes and scale of a graph.”

More precise

“Students can identify the variables but cannot determine the interval represented between labeled points.”

Interactive Problem Size Check

Responsible AI and accessibility checkpoint

Before leaving the problem-definition stage

  • Describe the difficulty without labeling or diagnosing the learner.
  • Use evidence from interviews, observation, or work—not stereotypes.
  • Remove identifying details from examples.
  • Include accessibility needs as design conditions, not fixed learner identities.
  • Ask whether the problem itself needs to be reframed.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
