Five-Lesson Curriculum

Lesson 2: Diagnose the Learning Challenge

Essential question: What exactly is difficult about this learning task?

Lesson 1 Lesson 2 Lesson 3 Lesson 4 Lesson 5
Learning goals
  • Distinguish a broad difficulty from a specific learning challenge.
  • Break a STEM task into component knowledge, skills, procedures, and reasoning.
  • Use evidence from observation, work, or interview to define the challenge.
Suggested outputs
  • Peer interview
  • Broad-to-specific challenge sort
  • Task component map
  • Challenge statement

Learning-science concepts

MetacognitionTask analysisPrior knowledgeWorking memorySelf-monitoring

AI-literacy concepts

Problem framingPrompt specificityHuman judgment before AI useAvoiding false diagnosis
Suggested lesson flow
  1. Launch the essential question with a concrete example.
  2. Introduce the core learning-science and AI-literacy ideas.
  3. Engage students in an active sort, model, interview, design task, or user test.
  4. Create a student artifact that becomes part of the final project.
  5. Use an exit reflection or advisor checkpoint to prepare for the next lesson.
Adaptation note: This lesson can be taught in a classroom, small group, virtual club, or one-on-one tutoring setting. Adjust examples, reading level, and discussion structure for the age group.
Diagnostic evidence

Sources students may use

Peer interview

Ask what the learner is trying to do, where confusion begins, what has been tried, and what support is helpful.

Work sample

Identify where reasoning, procedures, or explanations break down.

Observation

Watch what the learner does before, during, and after becoming stuck.

Strong diagnosis: “The learner can identify variables but has difficulty deciding which quantities should be compared when selecting an equation.”
