Hunter College Pathways

From high school learning and League participation to Hunter College opportunities.

Hunter College is part of the City University of New York and serves students from across New York City through undergraduate, graduate, professional, and research programs. The League can help students see how the skills they build in school—AI literacy, research, collaboration, communication, and responsible design—connect to college study and future STEM pathways.

About Hunter College

A public college with broad academic and research opportunities

Academic breadth

Hunter offers study across the arts and sciences, education, health professions, social work, public health, and other fields. Students can connect interests in AI and STEM learning to computer science, mathematics, psychology, education, neuroscience, data analysis, and related areas.

Urban access

Located in New York City, Hunter provides access to a diverse student body, schools, cultural institutions, community organizations, internships, and research partnerships.

Research and mentoring

Faculty laboratories, academic departments, student organizations, and campus centers can expose students to research methods, human-centered technology, educational innovation, and college-level problem solving.

High school to Hunter

How students can prepare during high school

Step 1

Build a strong academic foundation

Take challenging coursework in mathematics, science, computer science, writing, and social science. The ability to analyze evidence, explain reasoning, and communicate clearly is valuable across college majors.

Step 2

Develop a record of sustained participation

Clubs, research projects, tutoring, robotics, debate, community service, internships, and independent projects can show curiosity, persistence, teamwork, and leadership.

Step 3

Explore possible fields of study

Students should learn about majors, required courses, college costs, financial aid, academic supports, and career pathways before applying.

Step 4

Prepare for the CUNY application process

Students should work with counselors and families to review application requirements, deadlines, transcripts, financial-aid forms, and any program-specific expectations.

Step 5

Use college supports after enrollment

Advising, tutoring, faculty office hours, peer networks, student clubs, internships, and research opportunities can help students build belonging and progress toward a degree.

A possible League-to-Hunter sequence

Connections that can deepen as the League grows

School-based League participation

Students complete the five lessons, identify a real STEM learning challenge, and create and test an AI-supported learning aid.

Local and cross-school showcases

Students present their work, receive feedback, compare approaches, and develop stronger explanations of learning, evidence, and limitations.

Hunter College campus exposure

Students may attend a campus event, hear from faculty and undergraduates, learn about majors, and connect their project to college-level fields of study.

Researcher and near-peer interaction

Hunter faculty, staff, and students can participate in interviews, design critiques, demonstrations, or mentoring activities that preserve student ownership.

Advanced project development

Students may extend a prototype, investigate a research question, collect more systematic evidence, or develop a community-focused learning project.

College application and transition

Students can describe their League work as evidence of sustained interest, initiative, collaboration, ethical reasoning, and problem solving while also receiving guidance about the broader CUNY and Hunter application process.

Return as a mentor or leader

Hunter students and alumni who participated in similar experiences can support new teams, contribute to showcases, and strengthen the League network.

Fields students may explore

Academic areas connected to League work

Computer science and AI

Programming, algorithms, data, machine learning, robotics, software systems, privacy, and human-centered computing.

Education and learning sciences

Teaching, curriculum, assessment, special education, educational psychology, and technology-supported learning.

Psychology and cognition

Memory, attention, motivation, development, decision-making, and human behavior.

Mathematics and data

Quantitative reasoning, statistics, modeling, data analysis, and evidence-based decision making.

Neuroscience and health

Connections among the brain, behavior, learning, development, and human well-being.

Communication and public impact

Writing, presentation, storytelling, design, ethics, and community engagement.

Important: Participation in the League does not guarantee admission to Hunter College. The pathway is intended to help students build relevant skills, understand possible fields of study, become familiar with college environments, and make more informed choices about postsecondary education.
