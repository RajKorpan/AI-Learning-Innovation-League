Hunter College Pathways

From high school learning and League participation to college study, research, and leadership.

Hunter College is part of the City University of New York. The League can help students connect AI literacy, learning science, research, collaboration, and public communication to possible college majors and STEM pathways.

Pathways from high school to Hunter

Build an academic foundation

Develop strong reasoning, writing, mathematics, science, computing, and communication skills.

Participate deeply

Sustained clubs, research, tutoring, robotics, service, and design projects can build initiative and leadership.

Explore fields of study

Investigate computer science, education, psychology, mathematics, data, neuroscience, and related programs.

Prepare for CUNY applications

Work with counselors and families to review current requirements, deadlines, transcripts, financial aid, and program expectations.

Use college supports

Advising, tutoring, faculty office hours, peer networks, internships, student organizations, and research can support persistence.

Team leadershipCollaboration, project management, and mentoringStudent organizations, peer mentoring, campus leadership

A possible League-to-Hunter sequence

  1. Complete a school-based League project.
  2. Present at a local or cross-school showcase.
  3. Participate in a Hunter College campus exposure event when available.
  4. Receive design feedback from faculty, staff, or near-peer mentors.
  5. Extend the project through more systematic research or advanced development.
  6. Use the experience to understand majors, applications, and college expectations.
  7. Return as a mentor, judge, researcher, or club leader.
Important: League participation does not guarantee admission to Hunter College. It may help students build skills, explore fields, understand college environments, and make informed postsecondary choices.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
