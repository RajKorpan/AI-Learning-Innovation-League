Full Hunter College Pathway

From a school club to research, college, and STEM leadership.

The pathway shows how students can progress from introductory League participation to deeper mentoring, campus experiences, research exposure, and future college opportunities.

Pathway overview

A sequence that can grow over time

Stage 1

School or community club

Complete the five lessons, identify a real STEM learning challenge, build a responsible AI-supported learning aid, and test it with peers.

Stage 2

Local showcase

Present the learning goal, challenge diagnosis, strategy, prototype, test evidence, revisions, and limitations to teachers, families, and community guests.

Stage 3

Cross-school League event

Exchange feedback with students from other schools, join design challenges, and receive recognition for learning science, safety, accessibility, collaboration, and revision.

Stage 4

Hunter College campus experience

Attend a campus event, meet faculty and student researchers, tour relevant spaces, and connect project work to college study and STEM careers.

Stage 5

Researcher and near-peer mentoring

Work with Hunter College students, educators, and researchers through interviews, design reviews, technical workshops, and structured mentoring.

Stage 6

Advanced project or summer pathway

Develop a more substantial prototype, data-literacy experience, research question, or community learning project with stronger documentation and evaluation.

Stage 7

College and leadership opportunities

Return as a mentor, club leader, judge, student researcher, or undergraduate contributor and help new teams enter the pathway.

Possible Hunter experiences

Programs and supports to develop

Researcher interviews

Short conversations about AI, learning, data, robotics, research methods, and college pathways.

TIER Lab connections

Examples of trustworthy, explainable, ethical, and human-centered technology design.

Data-literacy storytelling

A project connection using robots, data, and narrative to help students interpret and communicate evidence.

Campus showcase

A public event where school teams demonstrate projects and receive feedback from peers, families, educators, and researchers.

Near-peer mentors

Hunter College students support design reviews, technical questions, and college awareness without taking over student work.

Advanced summer experience

A future Development-phase opportunity inspired by university research and apprenticeship programs but tailored to the League.

Development note: The current project can pilot small, manageable Hunter connections. A full research-apprenticeship pathway is better suited to a later Development phase with additional time, staffing, partnerships, and infrastructure.
Equity and access

Pathway design commitments

  • Participation should not depend on prior coding experience.
  • Students should have multiple entry points and roles.
  • Travel, scheduling, device access, and family communication must be considered.
  • Near-peer mentoring should supplement—not replace—school-based support.
  • Advanced opportunities should have transparent eligibility and selection processes.
  • Students should be able to return as mentors and leaders.
