# Build Your Club Plan: Rule Logic

The planner runs entirely in the browser. It does not use generative AI, transmit form data, or save entries. It assembles a plan from deterministic recommendations and combination checks.

## Inputs that affect recommendations

- **Number of students:** calculates the number and approximate size of project teams; changes role assignments and adult-capacity warnings.
- **Grade band:** changes scaffolding, session design, account guidance, independence, and mixed-age participation recommendations.
- **Student experience:** changes the amount of modeling, allowable scope, prototype expectations, and testing rigor.
- **Implementation format:** changes team structure, teacher-client coordination, virtual participation, lunch-club scope, or boot-camp prework.
- **Schedule:** selects the five-, six-, ten-week, or semester calendar.
- **Meeting length:** creates a recommended meeting routine and affects compression warnings.
- **Meeting frequency:** changes the delivery pattern and estimated contact time.
- **Adult facilitation:** changes recommended adult roles and the number of simultaneous teams that can be supported.
- **Teacher-client status:** changes whether students can move beyond interview practice and generates recruitment or scheduling priorities.
- **Technology level:** changes product recommendations, platform checks, technical-support requirements, and low-tech fallback guidance.
- **Family communication:** changes the communication actions required before interviews, testing, account use, or public sharing.
- **Sharing-event format:** creates school, virtual, family/community, or decision-deadline recommendations.
- **Available supports:** checks for meeting space, devices, prototyping materials, testers, family reviewers, technical support, additional adults, translation, accessibility coordination, transportation/event support, and protected project time.
- **Stated access and logistics needs:** matches explicit words to possible language, reading, visual, hearing, motor, cognitive/participation, device, transportation, scheduling, and food-access actions. These are suggestions to review with the participant and do not make diagnoses.

## Combination checks

Examples include:

- One-on-one format with more than one student
- Virtual delivery without devices and internet
- Advanced application development without technical support
- Upper-elementary students using account-based building platforms without confirmed requirements
- Five 45-minute meetings for students who are new to design and AI
- Large numbers of simultaneous teams with one advisor
- A semester schedule paired with a single intensive day
- A boot camp without an identified teacher-client problem
- A family/community event before family communication is sent
- In-person formats without an identified meeting space
- Compressed schedules without protected project work time
- Technology-dependent plans without devices
- Stated language, accessibility, or transportation needs without corresponding supports

## Generated plan sections

1. Plan status and explanation
2. Configuration summary and estimated contact time
3. Recommended team and rotating-role structure
4. Meeting routine and delivery pattern
5. Format-, grade-, experience-, and adult-specific recommendations
6. Before-students-begin checklist
7. First-meeting plan
8. Adapted calendar with artifacts and advisor checkpoints
9. Technology and product recommendations
10. Teacher-client, family, and testing recommendations
11. Accessibility and participation recommendations
12. Sharing-event recommendations
13. Prioritized required, strong, and helpful actions
14. Assumptions that still need local confirmation
