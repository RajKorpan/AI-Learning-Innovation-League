Student Participation Guide

What students will do, how teams work, and what participation may involve.

Most students will work as one team on a shared learning tool. Team members may hold more than one role and should rotate roles so technical work does not become a fixed hierarchy.

Core participation

Learn

Complete the five-lesson foundation on human learning, AI literacy, design thinking, evidence, safety, and accessibility.

Interview

Practice an empathy interview, interview a teacher-client, and listen to peer learners with permission.

Research

Analyze a task, locate credible sources, verify AI-supported research, and record evidence strength.

Design and build

Generate several options and create a low-resolution prototype. The product may or may not use AI.

Test and revise

Conduct staged tests, observe user behavior, identify unexpected outcomes, and improve the design.

Share

Participate in a showcase or presentation and explain the educational justification, evidence, and limitations.

Approximate time commitment

FormatTypical commitment
Five-week pilotOne focused meeting each week plus limited team work between sessions
Six-week cycleOne meeting per week with a dedicated showcase or reflection session
Ten-week clubMore time for interviews, research, testing, and revision
SemesterMultiple iterations, deeper evidence, and more developed products
Boot campPre-work plus an intensive design, test, and share experience

Research and feedback activities

During a planning pilot, students may be invited to complete surveys, interviews, or focus groups about their experience. These activities should follow the project’s approved procedures, required permissions, and clear explanations of privacy and voluntary participation.

  • Do not enter identifying information into AI tools.
  • Ask before recording interviews.
  • Use respectful, nonjudgmental language.
  • Allow interviewees and testers to stop.
  • Remove identifying details before sharing work.
  • Ask an advisor when a situation raises safety or privacy concerns.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
