Session 5 · Test + Share

Try it and improve it

Back to guide

Mission

Help students run one useful test, make an evidence-based revision, and prepare a clear share-out story.

Key question: What did the learner actually do, and what will we change because of it?

Before the session

  • Have the learning goal, success indicator, and current prototype available.
  • Confirm who may test the prototype and what permissions or privacy boundaries apply.
  • Prepare an accessible backup way to demonstrate the project.
  • Know the difference between evidence that the activity was usable and evidence that learning may have improved.

Watch for

  • Treating “they liked it” or “they finished” as proof of learning.
  • Leading the tester or rescuing them too quickly.
  • AI doing the important thinking instead of the learner.
  • Claims in the showcase that go beyond what the team actually observed.

If students get stuck

  • “What would the learner do differently if this actually helped?”
  • “What did you see, hear, or measure?”
  • “Did the learner do the thinking, or did the tool do it for them?”
  • “What is one change you can make because of this test?”

Plan and run one test5–25

Students choose the lightest useful test, decide what to observe, run it when appropriate, and record what actually happened.

Check together32–35

What students should produce

  • Focused test question
  • Test observations
  • At least one evidence-based revision
  • Revised prototype/version
  • share-out story
  • Focused demo plan
  • Accessible backup or alternative

Quick check

  • Can students distinguish usability from learning evidence?
  • Can they name one change caused by testing?
  • If AI is involved, did the learner still do the important thinking?
  • Do share-out claims match the evidence?
  • Can the team explain what it would improve next?
