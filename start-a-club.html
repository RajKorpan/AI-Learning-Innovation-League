Start and Run a Club

Choose a format, recruit participants, follow the design cycle, and begin another iteration.

The same human-centered process can be adapted to one-on-one tutoring, small groups, classrooms, virtual clubs, lunch clubs, boot camps, showcase days, and longer cycles.

Ten-part implementation sequence

  1. Before you begin: confirm purpose, advisor, permissions, technology, safety, accessibility, space, and time.
  2. Choose a format: one-on-one, small group, classroom, virtual, lunch, boot camp, or semester.
  3. Recruit students: explain the shared team product, time, interviews, testing, reflection, and showcase.
  4. Connect with teachers and families: recruit teacher-clients and communicate privacy, logistics, accommodations, and feedback roles.
  5. Form the team: assign and rotate five simplified roles.
  6. Select a schedule: five-week pilot, six-week, ten-week, or semester.
  7. Follow the design cycle: empathize, define, research, ideate, prototype, and test iteratively.
  8. Test projects: self-test, peer test, teacher-client review, learner test, and revised test.
  9. Prepare a showcase: select the appropriate competition level and product track.
  10. Reflect and begin another cycle: decide whether to redefine, redesign, extend, or stop.

Implementation formats

One-on-one tutoring

A teacher guides one student. Add teacher-client, family, peer, or virtual feedback so the student designs for another learner rather than only for themself.

Small group

Students share one product, rotate roles, conduct peer interviews, and test across teams.

Classroom integration

Teach the five lessons in class and use class, independent, or club time for project development.

Virtual club

Use shared documents, breakout groups, clear agendas, accessible tools, and privacy-safe examples.

Lunch or short-cycle club

Reduce scope to one component and one prototype question. Use short mini-lessons and templates.

Boot camp

Complete pre-work, then rotate through empathy synthesis, task analysis, evidence, prototyping, and critique stations.

Showcase day

Use an existing project for final testing, revision, presentation rehearsal, and community feedback.

Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
