Start a Club

Move from idea to first meeting.

The League can begin as a one-on-one tutoring experience, a small group, a classroom extension, a lunch club, an afterschool club, or a virtual cohort.

Club readiness checklist

Before you launch

People and purpose

  • Identify an advisor or coaching team
  • Choose the student age range
  • Decide whether students work individually or in teams
  • Clarify the club’s learning and design goals
  • Identify possible mentors or community partners

Setting and schedule

  • Choose in-person, virtual, or hybrid delivery
  • Set meeting length and frequency
  • Reserve the room or online meeting space
  • Choose a short-cycle, ten-week, or semester roadmap
  • Identify a possible showcase date

Technology and policy

  • Confirm device and internet access
  • Review school AI and acceptable-use policies
  • Check platform age and account requirements
  • Confirm privacy and data-handling procedures
  • Prepare a no-code or paper-prototype backup option

Students and families

  • Recruit students
  • Share a family overview
  • Collect required permissions
  • Explain privacy and responsible-use expectations
  • Invite families to a final sharing event
Choose a format

Flexible implementation models

One-on-one tutoring

Best when a teacher works with one student and uses structured interviews, reflection, and external feedback to add a user-centered design perspective.

Small-group club

Best for peer interviews, team roles, collaborative design, testing, and presentation.

Class plus club

Teach the five lessons during class and use club time for building, testing, revision, and sharing.

Virtual cohort

Bring students together across schools for peer interviews, design reviews, tool testing, and final presentations.

Lunch or short-cycle

Use mini-lessons and tightly structured project steps across several short meetings.

Boot camp day

Combine instruction, design, building, testing, and showcase preparation in an intensive event.

First meeting agenda

A practical launch session

10 minutes
Welcome and purpose
Explain that students will create AI-supported tools that help learners think, practice, explain, and reflect.
15 minutes
Human learning vs. AI
Discuss how human understanding differs from AI-generated output.
15 minutes
Explore examples
Review one strong and one weak AI learning interaction.
15 minutes
Identify interests
Students name STEM topics and learning challenges they care about.
5 minutes
Next step
Preview the five-lesson sequence and the final student product.
Advisor reminder: A club does not need a competition structure at the beginning. The first goal is to develop a workable curriculum, support student-designed prototypes, and collect evidence about feasibility and engagement.
