Five-Week Pilot

One small team, one teacher-client need, one shared learning tool.

The pilot compresses the full design cycle while preserving empathy, task analysis, evidence, low-resolution prototyping, realistic testing, reflection, and educational justification.

Before Week 1

Advisor preparation

Confirm people, permissions, teacher-client, team, technology options, family communication, safety, accessibility, evaluation, and showcase format.

Week 1

Understand and Listen — Empathize

Human learning and AI mini-lesson; practice interview; teacher-client interview; peer learner interview; synthesis.

Outputs: empathy notes, changed assumptions, candidate needs.

Week 2

Diagnose and Research — Define

Problem Size Check; task analysis; smallest meaningful component; learning progression; evidence record.

Outputs: precise problem, task map, evidence claims, design implications.

Week 3

Imagine and Prototype — Ideate + Prototype

Generate at least three product types; decide whether AI adds value; create a low-resolution prototype.

Outputs: concept comparison, prototype, test question, safety checkpoint.

Week 4

Test and Improve — Test

Team self-test, peer-team test, teacher-client review, learner test when approved, revision, and revised test.

Outputs: observation record, changed assumptions, revision log, version comparison.

Week 5

Share and Reflect

Complete rubric, educational justification, presentation, team reflection, family/community feedback, and next-cycle decision.

Outputs: presentation, final prototype, evidence summary, limitations, reflection.

Five-student role model

RolePrimary responsibility
Interview and Empathy LeadPrepare conversations, follow stories, synthesize needs
Learning Research LeadTask analysis, evidence record, progression, myth check
Design and Prototype LeadGenerate options, organize prototype, document versions
Testing and Evidence LeadPlan realistic tests, observe behavior, connect evidence to revision
Documentation and Presentation LeadDesign notebook, contribution log, rubric, educational justification, presentation

Roles should rotate or overlap. Every student should understand the full project and be able to explain central decisions.

Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
