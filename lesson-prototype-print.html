Printable Facilitation Guide

Session 4 · Prototype

Build something small

Back to guide

Mission

Help students turn one promising idea into the smallest version that can answer an important design question.

Key question: What is the least we can build to learn whether this idea helps?

Before the session

  • Have the selected strategy, learning goal, resources, and shortlisted idea available.
  • Gather quick-build materials before the session.
  • Know which AI platforms, if any, students are permitted to use.
  • Plan a low-tech alternative for any technology-dependent prototype.

Watch for

  • Teams polishing before they have tested the core interaction.
  • Assuming a more technical build is automatically better.
  • AI giving answers that the learner should be thinking through.
  • Prototypes that require inaccessible accounts, formats, or devices.

If students get stuck

  • “What is one question you need this prototype to answer?”
  • “Could paper, role-play, or a mock conversation answer that question first?”
  • “What important thinking must the learner still do?”

Activities · 1 × 45-minute meeting

See what projects can look like0–7

Use one or two examples to show that strong projects can be physical, printable, conversational, hybrid, or digital. Do not review every example.

Student activity: student-prototype.html#example-tools

Students produce: a clearer sense of possible product forms

Plan the smallest prototype7–25

Students choose a build direction and specify the smallest testable version. Keep the focus on what they need to learn from the prototype.

Student activity: student-prototype.html#build-planner

Students produce: a minimum prototype plan

Check the learning design25–32

Students work through the design gate. If a gate fails, stop and fix the design rather than pushing forward.

Student activity: student-prototype.html#design-check

Students produce: a prototype that passes the core learning-design checks

Design the AI behavior only when needed32–40

Only teams building a reusable AI learning tool use Prompt Builder. Other teams use this time to build or refine their low-tech/physical prototype.

Student activity: student-prototype.html#prompt-builder

Students produce: an AI prompt draft when relevant, or a working non-AI prototype

Check together40–45

Ask students to explain why this product form fits the learning goal and what the learner still has to think or do.

Student activity: student-prototype.html#advisor-check

Students produce: a facilitator-approved prototype plan

Build the smallest versionSession 2 · 0–25

Teams build with paper, objects, role-play, approved AI, or other appropriate materials.

Check togetherSession 2 · 38–45

Confirm the prototype is small, accessible enough to test, and still makes the learner do the important thinking.

Students produce: facilitator-approved prototype

What students should produce

  • Chosen build direction
  • One prototype question
  • Minimum prototype plan
  • Working low-resolution prototype
  • Learning-design check
  • Prompt draft only when conversational AI is part of the design

Quick check

  • Is the prototype small enough to change quickly?
  • Does the learner still do the important thinking?
  • Is AI purposeful rather than decorative?
  • Could the team test the key interaction without building more?
