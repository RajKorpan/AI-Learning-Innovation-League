Printable Facilitation Guide

Session 2 · Define

Help students use their Empathy Map to find one specific place where learning starts to get difficult.

Back to guide

Key question: Where does it first get hard, and what smaller part can we actually design for?

Prepare

  • Have students bring their Empathy Maps.
  • Preview the downloadable Task Map.
  • Have one worked example of a large task broken into smaller steps.
  • Be ready to offer paper, cards, or another visual way to sequence steps.

Watch for

  • Broad labels such as “bad at math.”
  • Treating a best guess as a diagnosis.
  • Skipping the steps immediately before the difficulty.
  • Choosing a product before the focused learning challenge is clear.

If students get stuck, try asking

  • What happens immediately before it gets hard?
  • What can the learner already do?
  • What evidence makes you think this is the first meaningful change?
  • Could an access barrier explain what you are seeing?

Quick check

  • Is the challenge small enough for one prototype?
  • Can students point to evidence for the focus?
  • Is the language a best guess to test rather than a diagnosis?
  • Is the success sign something you could actually observe?
