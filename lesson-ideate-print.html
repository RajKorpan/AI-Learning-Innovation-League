Session 3 · Ideate

Help students choose a learning move that fits the problem and generate several genuinely different ways to create it.

Back to guide

Key question: What should the learner actually do to practice the part that is difficult?

Prepare

  • Have the focused learning challenge and success sign available.
  • Preview the learning-move cards and the downloadable Idea Sheet.
  • Gather ordinary materials students can sketch or build with.
  • Be ready to keep the learning strategy separate from the product or tool.

Watch for

  • Choosing a chatbot before deciding what the learner needs to do.
  • Treating one strategy as the only correct answer.
  • Generating one idea and defending it immediately.
  • Skipping the required low/no-tech idea.

If students get stuck, try asking

  • What should the learner actually do?
  • What could make that action happen without a screen?
  • How is this idea meaningfully different from the last one?
  • Which direction best fits the evidence, not the technology?

Generate varied ideas

1 × 45 min13–25
2 × 45 minMeeting 1 · 22–45

Students use the Idea Sheet or sticky notes to generate low/no-tech, social, physical/visual, digital/AI, and wild-card ideas.

Student activity: student-ideate.html#idea-board

Students produce: five meaningfully different idea types

Use AI to stretch the ideas

1 × 45 min25–32
2 × 45 minMeeting 2 · 0–15

Students select available resources and generate a prompt asking AI for twists and alternatives—not the best answer.

Student activity: student-ideate.html#resource-inventor

Students produce: additional possibilities worth discussing

Shortlist two directions

1 × 45 min32–40
2 × 45 minMeeting 2 · 15–32

Students compare ideas on learning fit, evidence fit, buildability, and access, then save a preferred direction and optional backup.

Student activity: student-ideate.html#shortlist

Students produce: a preferred direction + backup if useful

Check together

1 × 45 min40–45
2 × 45 minMeeting 2 · 32–45

Check that the strategy fits, the idea set includes real variety, and the preferred direction preserves learner thinking.

Student activity: student-ideate.html#advisor-check

Students produce: a checked direction ready for Prototype

Quick check

  • Can students explain the strategy without relying on jargon?
  • Did they generate a genuinely low/no-tech idea?
  • Are they choosing based on fit rather than technical impressiveness?
  • Can they explain how AI contributes to the project or build process?
