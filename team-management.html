Team Facilitation

Five simplified roles, shared responsibility, and rotation.

For a five-student pilot, the five roles align naturally with team size. In smaller teams, members may hold more than one role. Rotate responsibilities so roles do not become fixed hierarchies.

Five team roles

Interview and Empathy Lead

Prepares starter questions, follows stories, protects permission and privacy, synthesizes needs, and checks that design remains grounded in people.

Learning Research Lead

Coordinates task analysis, source verification, evidence strength, learning myths, progression mapping, and design implications.

Design and Prototype Lead

Organizes ideation, compares product types, creates low-resolution prototypes, and records versions without controlling all technical work.

Testing and Evidence Lead

Defines test questions, stages tests, separates user statements from behavior, and connects observations to revisions.

Documentation and Presentation Lead

Maintains the design notebook, contribution log, rubric evidence, educational justification, and accessible presentation.

Group routine

  1. Review the current design mode and the evidence needed.
  2. Assign or rotate roles.
  3. Complete one focused task.
  4. Pause for a safety, accessibility, and learning-goal check.
  5. Receive one strength, one question, and one evidence-based critique.
  6. Record decisions, assumptions, versions, and contributions.
  7. Identify the next action and whether the team must move backward.

Inclusive participation

  • Offer oral, written, visual, asynchronous, and assisted ways to contribute.
  • Separate technical building from educational decision-making.
  • Use clear task chunks, models, checklists, and flexible pacing.
  • Rotate high-status tasks such as building and presenting.
  • Use accessible tools and low-tech alternatives.
  • Reduce scope instead of excluding a team member.
  • Critique the design, not the person.
  • Record individual and shared contributions.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
