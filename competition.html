Competition and Showcases

The League recognizes different stages of work and different product types. Empathy, evidence, alignment, testing, accessibility, and reflection matter more than technical sophistication.

On this pageLevelsProduct tracksRubricEducational justificationPresentationEvents

Three competition levels

Level 1: Prospective Prototyping

Present: empathy findings, defined problem, task analysis, learning-science review, proposed design, and low-resolution prototype.

Level 2: Product Showcase

Present: completed or partial tool, testing evidence, feedback, revisions, and early evidence of usefulness.

Level 3: Educational Justification

Explain: problem choice, evidence, product format, productive struggle, feedback, impact, limitations, and next steps.

Product tracks

AI-assisted non-AI tool

AI may support research, design, or content, while the final tool is paper, physical, visual, game-based, or activity-based.

Gem or chatbot

The final tool uses conversational AI for practice, reflection, debate, question decomposition, or error analysis.

Advanced custom AI application

The team builds a custom application when the added technology clearly serves the learning goal.

Competition rubric

CategoryWhat strong work shows
Empathy and User UnderstandingRich teacher-client and learner evidence; assumptions changed.
Problem DefinitionPrecise, observable, right-sized, and evidence supported.
Task AnalysisKnowledge, actions, prerequisites, decisions, errors, load, and bottleneck.
Learning-Science EvidenceCredible sources, evidence strength, myths checked, implications explicit.
Alignment Between Need and DesignFeatures support the learner action and teacher-client need.
Quality of PrototypePurposeful, testable, focused, and appropriate to the stage.
Feedback and RevisionMeaningful evidence-linked revisions and changed assumptions.
Usability and AccessibilityVaried users and access needs considered; alternatives provided.
Responsible AI UseAI role justified; privacy, bias, accuracy, agency, limits, and verification addressed.
Testing and Evidence of ImpactRealistic tests; user behavior and target learning evidence documented.
Educational JustificationCoherent defense of the problem, evidence, tool, struggle, feedback, impact, and limits.
Team ReflectionSpecific learning, changed assumptions, contributions, conflict, and next cycle.
Presentation and CommunicationClear, accessible, evidence-rich, privacy-safe, and shared across the team.
Technical sophistication is not a scoring category by itself. A paper tool can score highly.

Educational justification

  1. Why did the team select this teacher-client and learner problem?
  2. What is the smallest meaningful component?
  3. What learning-science evidence informed the design?
  4. Why was this product format appropriate?
  5. How did the tool preserve productive struggle?
  6. How did feedback work and fade?
  7. How did testing change the product or problem?
  8. What evidence suggests usefulness?
  9. What responsible AI and accessibility decisions were made?
  10. What limitations remain?

Presentation best practices

  • Lead with the human need, not the platform.
  • Use one clear demonstration and a backup.
  • Show empathy, task analysis, evidence, and revision visually.
  • Share speaking roles.
  • Explain what the learner still has to think or do.
  • Describe limitations honestly.
  • Protect privacy.
  • Use readable text, contrast, captions, and verbal descriptions.
