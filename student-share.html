Step 4

Plan the demo

Prepare a focused demonstration

1Set the challenge

Give enough context to understand the learning problem without identifying the learner.

2Show one meaningful interaction

Use a prepared example that reveals the learning strategy and the learner's role.

3Explain the evidence

Say what testing showed, what it did not show, and what you changed.

4Explain AI honestly

If AI was used, explain its role, limits, and what you verified. If it was not used, explain why another form fit better.

5Have a backup

Use screenshots, a recorded interaction, paper prototype, or storyboard if the device or internet fails.

6Invite a useful question

Be ready to discuss the learning problem, design choices, testing, limitations, and next step.

Step 5

See ways to share your work

The Learning Showcase

Share what you discovered—not just a finished product

Your project does not need to look polished or be “done.” The most useful story shows the problem, what your team tried, what happened, and what you would improve next.

Live demonstrationLet people see one meaningful learner interaction.
Project storyShow the problem → breakdown → strategy → prototype → test → revision.
Peer feedbackAsk another team or visitor what they notice and what they would test next.
RecognitionCelebrate careful learning design, useful evidence, creativity, accessibility, and iteration—not technical complexity.
You can share work in progress.A good showcase explains what you learned from making and testing, including what did not work yet.

Student + advisor

Final review

Review the story, demo scenario, learner privacy, accessibility, technology backup, claims about impact, and speaking roles with your advisor before the Learning Showcase.

Accessibility check

Can the audience access the explanation and demonstration? Prepare readable visuals, clear spoken explanations, captions or transcripts when relevant, alternate formats, and a backup that does not depend on one device or internet connection.
