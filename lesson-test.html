Facilitation Guide

Mission

Help students run one useful test, make an evidence-based revision, and prepare an honest share-out of whatever progress is most useful to show.

Sharing does not require a polished product.

Students may center the share-out on what they learned, the problem they identified, an early prototype, a test and revision, or a more developed tool. The venue and date can be communicated separately.

Key questionWhat did the learner actually do, and what will we change because of it?
Use this guide when you need it

Prepare, then facilitate

Before the sessionStep 1
Step 1

Prepare

You do not need to reteach the student activity here. Prepare the conditions that let students do the thinking themselves.

Before students arrive

  • Have the learning goal, success indicator, and current prototype available.
  • Confirm who may test the prototype and what permissions or privacy boundaries apply.
  • Prepare an accessible backup way to demonstrate the project.
  • Know the difference between evidence that the activity was usable and evidence that learning may have improved.
Step 2

Know what to watch for

Watch for

  • Treating “they liked it” or “they finished” as proof of learning.
  • Leading the tester or rescuing them too quickly.
  • AI doing the important thinking instead of the learner.
  • Claims in the showcase that go beyond what the team actually observed.

If students get stuck, try saying

  • “What would the learner do differently if this actually helped?”
  • “What did you see, hear, or measure?”
  • “Did the learner do the thinking, or did the tool do it for them?”
  • “What is one change you can make because of this test?”
Step 3

Preview the student path

The student pathway contains the actual activity, examples, tools, and response scaffolds. Open it before the session so you know what students will see.

Step 3

What students should produce

Students should have enough evidence to tell an honest story. They do not need polished final work, and the share-out can center on progress rather than a completed tool.

Session outputs

  • Focused test question
  • Test observations
  • At least one evidence-based revision
  • Revised prototype/version
  • Share-out story focused on the team’s most useful progress
  • Focused demo plan
  • Accessible backup or alternative
Step 4

Quick check

Use these questions to decide whether students are ready to move forward or need one more conversation/revision.

Check before moving on

  • Can students distinguish usability from learning evidence?
  • Can they name one change caused by testing?
  • If AI is involved, did the learner still do the important thinking?
  • Do the team’s share-out claims match the evidence?
  • Can the team explain what it would improve next?
