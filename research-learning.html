Research How People Learn

Connect the smallest meaningful component to credible evidence.

AI may help generate search terms, compare explanations, or summarize a source, but students must verify claims, record sources, judge evidence strength, and connect findings to the identified learner need.

Research questions

Skill development

How does the target knowledge or skill typically develop?

Misconceptions

Which errors are common, and what reasoning may produce them?

Practice

What type, sequence, spacing, or variability of practice is effective?

Memory and retrieval

What must be recalled, recognized, or reconstructed?

Feedback

What feedback is useful, and when should it occur?

Motivation and persistence

What supports agency, relevance, confidence, and recovery after error?

Metacognition and self-regulation

What should learners plan, monitor, reflect on, or change?

Transfer and prior knowledge

What prerequisites and varied contexts support flexible use?

Cognitive load and accessibility

What representations, task chunks, sensory access, or alternatives reduce unnecessary barriers?

Evidence strength

RatingMeaning
Strong research supportMultiple credible studies or research syntheses support the claim in relevant contexts.
Mixed or limited supportEvidence is incomplete, context dependent, or not directly matched to the learner and task.
Unsupported claimA source states the claim without adequate evidence, or AI generated it without a verifiable source.
Common learning mythA familiar claim—such as matching fixed visual, auditory, or kinesthetic learning styles—should not guide design as established science.

Responsible AI and accessibility checkpoint

Before leaving the research stage

  • Verify important claims with credible sources.
  • Check AI summaries against the original source.
  • Distinguish evidence from suggestion or brainstorming.
  • Do not upload private learner information.
  • Record uncertainty, limitations, and context.
Student AI Learning Innovation League

A human-centered learning-design program in which students investigate authentic learning challenges, use learning science, create and test learning tools, and improve them through evidence and feedback.
