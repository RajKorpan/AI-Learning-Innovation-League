# Pilot Website Usability Testing Guide

This file is for the project team, not a public website page. It is intended to guide the next round of real-user testing after the Stage 1–6 website revisions.

## Purpose

Test whether advisors and students can use the League website with minimal explanation from the development team. The goal is to find places where users hesitate, misunderstand the task, cannot locate the next action, or need more/less support.

Any collection or use of participant feedback for research purposes should follow the project's approved IRB/consent procedures.

## Advisor test

Ask an advisor to complete these tasks without a walkthrough:

1. Open **Advisors** and explain the League journey in their own words.
2. Build a club plan using a realistic school scenario.
3. Open Lesson 2 · Define and choose the 1 × 45-minute version.
4. Complete the **Before you facilitate** activity.
5. Find the student tool they would use in that lesson.
6. Explain what is core, what is optional, and what they would do for a student who needs more support.
7. On a phone, open the lesson menu and move to the next lesson.

Observe: time to first correct action, navigation errors, unexplained terminology, points where the advisor asks what to do next, and whether the 45-minute plan feels runnable.

## Student test

Use one realistic STEM learning challenge. Ask the student to:

1. Open **Students** and explain what they think the six stages mean.
2. Begin Empathize and identify what they are expected to save.
3. In Define, start the Learning Breakdown Mapper and use **I'm not sure / Help me figure this out** if appropriate.
4. Explain why the result is a hypothesis rather than a diagnosis.
5. In Ideate, complete a Strategy Explorer practice case and begin What Do You Have?.
6. In Prototype, compare the three build paths and explain why Advanced Build is optional.
7. Copy an approved-AI prompt and explain how they would leave the site, use it, and return.
8. In Test, identify the learning objective and the **Did the AI do too much?** check.
9. Copy or download a stage record.

Observe: reading load, whether one question at a time feels manageable, whether examples clarify difficult questions, whether buttons and outputs are usable on a phone, and whether the student understands why each tool exists.

## Phone-specific test

Repeat at least one student sequence on a phone:

- Open/close the main menu.
- Use the stage map.
- Complete a one-question-at-a-time tool.
- Select cards/buttons.
- Read generated output.
- Copy an AI prompt.
- Copy a stage summary.
- Download a stage record.
- Move Previous/Next.

Record any horizontal scrolling, clipped text, tiny tap targets, keyboard/zoom problems, or actions that require a laptop.

## Questions after the task

Keep the debrief short:

- What was easiest to understand?
- Where did you stop or hesitate?
- What did you expect to happen that did not happen?
- Was anything asking you to know something you did not know yet?
- Was there a place with too much text or too many choices?
- What would you change before another student/advisor used this?

## Prioritizing findings

Fix in this order:

1. Cannot complete the task / broken interaction.
2. Does not understand the learning-design purpose.
3. Does not know what to do next.
4. Accessibility or phone-use barrier.
5. Excess reading or confusing terminology.
6. Cosmetic preference.

Do not add new features merely because a user asks for them during a test. First determine whether the existing workflow can be made clearer or simpler.
