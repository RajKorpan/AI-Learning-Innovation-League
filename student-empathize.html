START HERE

Make something before you know enough

Your facilitator or partner will give you a two-minute build challenge. Make the first thing that comes to mind. Rough is the point.

Talk about what happened

  • What did you assume about the learner?
  • What did you assume was causing the difficulty?
  • What would you need to learn before trusting those assumptions?
  • Could an access need change what you assumed?

Keep the lesson, not the build: evidence comes before solutions.

INTERVIEW

Plan a short interview

Question Detective

Which question leaves more room for the learner to show you what is happening?

Strong questions ask the person to describe, show, or remember instead of confirming your theory.

REAL INTERVIEW

Conduct the interview

  • Ask for examples.
  • Follow what the person actually says.
  • Do not suggest the solution.
The template creates a notes area for each question you selected.
INDIVIDUAL WORK

Turn what you heard into evidence

Use your interview notes to complete the Empathy Map on your own first. Write direct evidence before your best guesses.

Empathy Map template

Evidence: goal, current actions, what was tried, what you heard or saw.
Best guesses: where it seems hard and what you still need to learn.

Download Empathy Map

Check your Empathy Map together

Talk with your facilitator or a partner. Use these questions:

  • Which parts came directly from what you heard or observed?
  • Which parts are still your best guess?
  • What important question is still unanswered?
  • Could an access need be affecting what you are seeing?
