Step 1

Your goalUnderstand the learning experience before proposing a solution.

Rule of this stage: The next two-minute build is only a warm-up. Make it quickly, notice your assumptions, then set it aside. For the real project, do not choose the solution yet—first learn what the learner is trying to do, what already works, where the experience becomes difficult, and what you still do not know.
Learning Lab

Getting an answer is not the same as learning. Look for something the person can remember, explain, decide, or do on their own.

AI can make examples, questions, and ideas. But it does not know this person. Treat AI ideas as things to check, not as facts about the learner.

Your facilitator is the adult guiding your team. They help you think through important choices without doing the project for you.

Step 2

Make something fast

2-minute build challenge

Build before you know the answer

Grab paper, an index card, sticky note, marker, or whatever is nearby. This is not your final solution. It is a fast reminder that the League is about making and testing, not only filling out forms.

Press the button, then give yourself only two minutes.

After two minutes: what did you notice?
  • What did your object or activity make the learner do?
  • What did you assume about what would help?
  • What would you need to know about a real learner before deciding whether this idea is useful?

Keep the energy, not the solution. Empathize now helps you learn whether your first idea addresses the real problem.

Core activity

Now conduct the real interview

  1. Use 3–5 starter questions.
  2. Ask for a specific example when something important comes up.
  3. Write short notes about what the person says or does.
  4. Do not decide what caused the difficulty yet.

When the interview is over, continue to Step 5.

Empathy Map check

Show your Empathy Map to your facilitator. Ask: Which parts came directly from the interview? Which parts are still our best guess? What should we investigate in Define?

Access check

Check access too. Could the directions, reading, language, sound, visuals, pace, device, or format be making the task harder?
