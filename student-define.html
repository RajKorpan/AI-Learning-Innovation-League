About learning: an incorrect answer can be the final symptom of an earlier breakdown. Decompose the task so you can distinguish prerequisites, decisions, representations, and strategies.

About AI: AI can help suggest possible task steps or hypotheses, but it can be incomplete or wrong. Your interviews, observations, work samples, and short learner tasks are the core evidence. Outside research is optional when a consequential claim needs verification.

Stuck? Talk the task through with an optional AI Task Breakdown Guide

Try mapping the task yourself first. If hidden steps are hard to see, this optional prompt asks an approved AI tool to question your reasoning and suggest a draft. You still decide what belongs in the map, and your advisor checks the hypothesis.

If you use the generated prompt
  1. Copy the prompt.
  2. Open the AI tool your advisor or school permits.
  3. Talk through the task without entering identifying learner information.
  4. Bring the AI's draft hypothesis back to this Task Map.
  5. Revise the map yourself and review it with your advisor. The AI draft is not a diagnosis or evidence.

Step 5

Check together

Do togetherDo the first thinking yourself, then make the decision with your advisor.
Student + advisor

Required checkpoint

Explain the task map, what the learner can already do, where the breakdown seems to begin, what evidence supports that hypothesis, the learning objective, and how you will know whether learning improved. Your advisor should challenge the reasoning—not simply approve the tool output.

Accessibility check

Could the apparent learning problem actually be an access problem—for example unclear directions, reading demand, language, visual presentation, motor interaction, pacing, attention, or device access? If so, redesign the environment or access first.
